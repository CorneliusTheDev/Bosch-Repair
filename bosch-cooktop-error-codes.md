## Error Code E0 – Internal Electronics Overheating

### What Does Bosch Cooktop Error Code E0 Mean?

The E0 error code on a Bosch cooktop indicates that the **internal electronics are overheating**. Bosch induction and electric cooktops sold in the United States contain sophisticated power electronics — including inverter circuits (for induction), relays, and a main control board — that generate heat during operation. These electronic components are cooled by one or more internal fans. When the temperature of the electronics exceeds the safe operating threshold, the cooktop reduces power or shuts off entirely and displays the E0 error.

This is a protective safety feature that prevents permanent damage to the expensive electronic components inside the cooktop. The E0 error is most commonly seen on Bosch induction cooktops, which use high-power inverter circuits that generate more internal heat than standard radiant electric cooktops.

When E0 appears, the cooktop may reduce the power level of one or more burners, or it may shut off all burners entirely. The display shows E0 until the electronics cool to a safe temperature, at which point the cooktop may resume operation or require a manual restart.

### Common Causes of the E0 Error Code

Internal electronics overheating can be caused by:

- **Blocked ventilation** – Bosch cooktops draw cooling air through intake vents (usually underneath the cooktop) and exhaust warm air through other vents. If these vents are blocked by cookware, towels, papers, or items stored in the cabinet below, the cooling system cannot function effectively.
- **Failed cooling fan** – The internal fan that moves air across the electronics can fail due to motor burnout, dust accumulation on the fan blades, or a seized bearing. Without active cooling, the electronics overheat quickly during high-power cooking.
- **Extended high-power cooking** – Running multiple burners at maximum power for extended periods (such as a large holiday meal) generates maximum internal heat. Even with functioning ventilation, the cooling system may reach its limits under sustained full-power operation.
- **High ambient temperature** – Cooking in a very hot kitchen (especially during summer without air conditioning) reduces the effectiveness of the cooling system since the incoming air is already warm.
- **Restricted cabinet airflow** – If the cooktop is installed in a tightly enclosed cabinet without adequate ventilation openings, the cabinet acts as a heat trap. Bosch installation instructions specify minimum clearance and ventilation requirements for the cabinet below the cooktop.
- **Dirty or dusty ventilation paths** – Over time, cooking grease, dust, and debris can accumulate on the ventilation grilles and internal air passages, restricting airflow.

### How to Troubleshoot the E0 Error Code

Follow these steps to address the overheating:

1. **Turn off all burners** – Remove cookware from the cooktop and turn off all burners to reduce heat generation.
2. **Let the cooktop cool** – Wait 15-30 minutes for the internal electronics to cool down. The E0 error should clear once temperatures return to normal.
3. **Check ventilation paths** – Look for the cooktop's intake and exhaust vents (check underneath the cooktop, at the edges, and in the cabinet below). Remove any items blocking these vents.
4. **Open the cabinet below** – Open the cabinet door or drawer beneath the cooktop to improve air circulation while the cooktop cools.
5. **Clean the ventilation grilles** – Wipe any visible grilles with a damp cloth to remove grease and dust buildup.
6. **Reduce power levels** – When you resume cooking, try using lower power levels and fewer burners simultaneously. If E0 appeared during maximum-power cooking, the cooling system may simply have been overwhelmed.
7. **Check the internal fan** – Listen for the cooling fan running when the cooktop is in use. On most Bosch cooktops, you can hear a soft whirring from below the surface during and after cooking. If you never hear the fan, it may have failed.

### When to Call a Professional

If E0 appears repeatedly during normal cooking (not extended high-power cooking), the internal cooling fan may need replacement or there may be an installation issue with cabinet ventilation. **Contact our Bosch cooktop repair team** for professional diagnosis using factory-certified parts.

## Error Code E1 – Burner Temperature Too High

### What Does Bosch Cooktop Error Code E1 Mean?

The E1 error code on a Bosch cooktop indicates that a **specific burner zone's temperature has exceeded the safe maximum**. Unlike E0 (which refers to electronics overheating), E1 relates to the actual cooking surface temperature of a particular burner. Each burner zone on a Bosch cooktop has a temperature sensor that monitors the surface temperature. When this sensor detects that the temperature has risen above the safe threshold, the burner is automatically shut off or reduced in power.

On Bosch induction cooktops, the E1 is particularly relevant because the cookware itself generates heat (via electromagnetic induction), and certain conditions can cause the cookware to overheat beyond what the cooktop considers safe. On radiant electric cooktops, E1 typically indicates the glass surface temperature near a specific burner has become dangerously hot.

This error appears on Bosch cooktops sold in the United States and usually affects only the specific burner that triggered the error — other burners may continue operating normally.

### Common Causes of the E1 Error Code

A burner zone overheating can result from:

- **Empty pan on the burner** – Leaving an empty pan (especially a thin, lightweight pan) on a burner at high power causes it to heat up extremely rapidly with no food to absorb the heat. The pan temperature can exceed 900°F in minutes.
- **Pan boiled dry** – If liquid in a pot evaporates completely and cooking continues, the pan temperature rises rapidly without the moderating effect of water or food.
- **Incorrect cookware** – On induction cooktops, cookware with thin, flat bases heats more efficiently than thick-bottomed cookware. Very thin induction-compatible pans can overshoot in temperature at high power settings.
- **Warped or damaged cookware** – Warped pans make inconsistent contact with the cooktop surface, creating hot spots and uneven heating that can trigger the temperature sensor.
- **High power for extended period** – Running a burner at maximum power for a long time without stirring or managing the cooking process can gradually push the surface temperature beyond the safe limit.
- **Temperature sensor fault** – A faulty burner temperature sensor can report temperatures higher than actual, causing a false E1 error.
- **Spillover on the surface** – Burned-on food or sugar spills on the glass cooktop surface near the temperature sensor can affect its accuracy.

### How to Troubleshoot the E1 Error Code

These steps address the burner overheating:

1. **Remove the cookware** – Immediately remove the pan from the affected burner zone. This eliminates the heat source and allows the burner to cool.
2. **Wait for cooling** – Allow the burner zone to cool for 10-15 minutes. The E1 error should clear once the surface temperature drops to a safe level. The "hot surface" indicator (usually an "H") will remain until the surface cools below the burn-risk temperature.
3. **Never heat an empty pan** – Always add food, oil, or liquid to your cookware before turning on the burner. Heating empty cookware is the single most common cause of E1.
4. **Use appropriate power levels** – Not all cooking needs maximum power. Use medium-high or medium for most sautéing and frying, reserving maximum power for bringing water to a boil.
5. **Use quality cookware** – Use heavy-bottomed, flat cookware appropriate for your cooktop type. For induction cooktops, use cookware specifically designed for induction with a thick, flat base for even heat distribution.
6. **Clean the cooktop surface** – Remove any burned-on residue near the burner zone, as it can interfere with the temperature sensor's accuracy.

### When to Call a Professional

If E1 appears during normal cooking with proper cookware and moderate power levels, the burner temperature sensor may be faulty. **Contact our Bosch cooktop repair team** for sensor testing and replacement using factory-certified parts.

## Error Code E2 – Power Supply Voltage Issue

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/cooktop/e2-power-supply-voltage-issue/`

### What Does Bosch Cooktop Error Code E2 Mean?

The E2 error code on a Bosch cooktop indicates a **power supply voltage issue**. Bosch cooktops sold in the United States are designed for 240V AC power (some compact models use 208V or 120V). The cooktop's control board monitors the incoming voltage, and when it detects that the voltage is too low, too high, or unstable, it triggers E2 to protect the electronics and heating systems.

Voltage irregularities are a serious concern for cooktops because they contain high-power electronics (especially induction cooktops with inverter circuits) that require stable, clean power to operate correctly. Low voltage can cause the cooktop to underperform (burners not reaching full power), while high voltage can damage electronic components and create safety hazards.

The E2 error will typically shut down the cooktop or reduce the available power until the voltage condition is resolved.

### Common Causes of the E2 Error Code

Power supply voltage issues can stem from:

- **Incorrect wiring** – If the cooktop was wired to the wrong voltage circuit during installation (such as connecting a 240V cooktop to a 120V circuit, or vice versa), the voltage will be completely wrong.
- **Loose electrical connections** – Loose wire connections at the cooktop junction box, the circuit breaker, or any junction point along the circuit can cause voltage drops, especially under the high current draw of a cooktop.
- **Undersized wiring** – If the electrical circuit uses wire that is too thin (wrong gauge) for the cooktop's current draw, voltage will drop significantly when the cooktop is operating at high power.
- **Utility voltage fluctuations** – Your local power company may be delivering voltage outside the normal range during peak demand periods.
- **Shared circuit** – Although code requires dedicated circuits for cooktops, an improper installation may share the circuit with other appliances, causing voltage drops when multiple devices draw power simultaneously.
- **Corroded breaker or connections** – Corrosion on circuit breaker contacts or wire terminals increases resistance and causes voltage drops under load.
- **Power phase loss** – On 240V cooktops, both hot legs must be present and delivering the correct voltage. If one leg is lost or damaged (often due to a tripped breaker on one pole or a utility-side issue), the cooktop receives half the expected voltage.

### How to Troubleshoot the E2 Error Code

Electrical troubleshooting should be done carefully:

1. **Check the circuit breaker** – Verify that the cooktop's circuit breaker is fully in the ON position. If it has tripped, reset it by pushing it fully to OFF and then back to ON. If it trips again immediately, there is a short circuit that requires professional diagnosis.
2. **Check for partial power** – If the cooktop turns on but displays E2 and burners have very low power, you may have a phase loss condition (only one leg of 240V is working).
3. **Verify the circuit** – Ensure the cooktop is on a dedicated circuit as specified in the installation guide. Check the breaker amperage against the cooktop's requirements (usually 30-50 amps depending on the model).
4. **Reset the cooktop** – Turn off the circuit breaker for 60 seconds and restore power. If E2 was caused by a temporary voltage fluctuation (brownout), it should clear after a reset.
5. **Check for ongoing voltage issues** – If you notice other symptoms in your home — dimming lights, flickering, slow appliances — you may have a broader utility voltage issue. Contact your power company.

### When to Call a Professional

Electrical work on 240V circuits requires a licensed electrician. If E2 persists after breaker reset, have an electrician test the voltage at the cooktop junction box under load. For cooktop-specific issues, **contact our Bosch cooktop repair team** for diagnostic testing.

## Error Code E3 – Cookware Detection Error (Induction)

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/cooktop/e3-cookware-detection-error/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Cooktop Error Code E3 – No Cookware Detected | Causes & Fixes

**Meta Description:** Bosch induction cooktop showing E3? This means no compatible cookware was detected. Learn why E3 appears and how to fix it on your Bosch induction cooktop.

**Keywords:** Bosch cooktop error code E3, Bosch induction cooktop no cookware detected, Bosch cooktop E3 fix, Bosch induction cooktop compatible pots, Bosch cooktop pan detection, Bosch induction cooktop repair, Bosch cooktop repair

---

### What Does Bosch Cooktop Error Code E3 Mean?

The E3 error code on a Bosch cooktop indicates a **cookware detection error**, and it is specific to Bosch induction cooktops. Induction cooktops work by generating an alternating magnetic field in the burner coil, which induces electrical currents in the base of ferromagnetic (iron-based) cookware, causing the cookware itself to heat up. The cooktop does not get hot directly — only the pan does.

For this to work, the cookware must be made of or contain a magnetic material (iron or magnetic stainless steel) and must be the right size for the burner zone. The E3 error appears when the cooktop's induction coil attempts to energize but cannot detect a suitable piece of cookware on the burner zone. The cooktop will not heat without a compatible pan in place — this is actually a safety feature that prevents the induction coil from running at high power with nothing to absorb the energy.

This error is found exclusively on Bosch induction cooktops sold in the United States and is one of the most commonly reported error codes, often because users are not yet familiar with induction-compatible cookware requirements.

### Common Causes of the E3 Error Code

The cookware detection error occurs because of:

- **Incompatible cookware** – Aluminum, copper, glass, ceramic, and non-magnetic stainless steel cookware will not work on induction cooktops because they are not ferromagnetic. This is the most common cause of E3 for new induction cooktop owners.
- **Cookware too small for the zone** – If the pan's base diameter is significantly smaller than the burner zone, the cooktop may not detect enough magnetic mass to register the cookware. Most Bosch induction zones require a minimum pan diameter (typically 4-5 inches for small zones and 7-8 inches for large zones).
- **Off-center placement** – The pan must be centered on the burner zone marking. A pan that is significantly off-center may not be detected by the induction coil's sensing circuit.
- **Warped pan bottom** – A pan with a concave or convex warped bottom does not make full contact with the glass surface. The air gap between the pan and the glass reduces the magnetic coupling to the point where the cooktop cannot detect the pan.
- **Empty or very lightweight pan** – Some very thin or lightweight induction pans may not be detected at low power settings because they present insufficient magnetic load to the sensor.
- **Pan with multi-layer base** – Some high-end cookware has a thin induction-compatible disc bonded to a non-magnetic body. If this disc is too thin or has delaminated, the cooktop may not detect it reliably.

### How to Troubleshoot the E3 Error Code

Resolve the cookware detection issue with these steps:

1. **Test with a known induction-compatible pan** – Use a simple magnet test: hold a refrigerator magnet to the bottom of your cookware. If the magnet sticks firmly, the pan is induction-compatible. If it slides off or barely holds, the pan will not work on induction.
2. **Center the pan** – Make sure the pan is centered on the burner zone markings printed on the glass surface.
3. **Use the right size pan** – Match the pan diameter to the burner zone size. A small pan on a large zone may not be detected. Bosch cooktops with FlexInduction zones are more flexible, but even these have minimum size requirements.
4. **Check for a flat bottom** – Place the pan on a flat surface and check for rocking. If the pan wobbles, its bottom is warped and it should be replaced.
5. **Clean the cooktop surface** – Ensure the glass surface is clean and free of residue. While this rarely causes E3, a very dirty surface can theoretically affect the sensor.
6. **Try a higher power level** – If a borderline pan is not detected at power level 1, try setting a higher power level. The increased coil current may detect the pan at higher sensitivity.

### When to Call a Professional

If E3 appears with known-compatible, properly sized, and properly placed cookware, the induction coil or its detection circuit may be faulty. **Contact our Bosch cooktop repair team** for professional induction system diagnosis.

## Error Code E4 – Residual Heat Warning Sensor Fault

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/cooktop/e4-residual-heat-sensor-fault/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Cooktop Error Code E4 – Residual Heat Sensor Fault | Causes & Fixes

**Meta Description:** Bosch cooktop displaying E4? This indicates a residual heat warning sensor fault. Learn the causes and solutions for the E4 sensor error on your Bosch cooktop.

**Keywords:** Bosch cooktop error code E4, Bosch cooktop residual heat sensor, Bosch cooktop E4 fix, Bosch cooktop hot surface indicator, Bosch cooktop H indicator, Bosch cooktop sensor failure, Bosch cooktop repair

---

### What Does Bosch Cooktop Error Code E4 Mean?

The E4 error code on a Bosch cooktop indicates a **residual heat warning sensor fault**. The residual heat indicator system uses temperature sensors to detect whether each burner zone is still hot after being turned off. On the display, this is shown as an "H" indicator for each zone that is still above a safe touch temperature (typically around 140°F / 60°C).

The E4 error is triggered when the control board detects that a residual heat sensor is providing out-of-range readings — either an open circuit, a short circuit, or values that are physically impossible given the burner's recent usage history.

This is an important safety system because the glass surface of a cooktop retains heat after the burner is turned off, and touching a hot zone can cause burns. Without a functioning residual heat sensor, the cooktop cannot warn users that the surface is still hot. The E4 error may prevent the affected burner zone from operating until the sensor is repaired, or it may allow cooking but disable the residual heat warning for that zone (depending on the model and severity).

### Common Causes of the E4 Error Code

The residual heat sensor can fault due to:

- **Sensor component failure** – The NTC thermistor used for residual heat sensing can fail due to age, thermal cycling fatigue, or manufacturing defect. This is the most common cause.
- **Sensor wiring damage** – The wires connecting the sensor to the control board can be damaged by heat, pinched during installation, or degraded over time.
- **Connector corrosion** – Spillovers that seep through the glass surface (at the edges or around mounting points) can corrode the sensor connectors.
- **Cracked cooktop glass affecting sensor** – If the glass cooktop surface is cracked near a sensor location, the crack can alter how heat conducts to the sensor, causing abnormal readings.
- **Control board input failure** – The analog input circuit on the control board that reads the sensor can develop a fault.

### How to Troubleshoot the E4 Error Code

Try these steps:

1. **Reset the cooktop** – Turn off the circuit breaker for 60 seconds and restore power. A temporary electronic anomaly may clear with a reset.
2. **Let the cooktop cool completely** – Ensure all zones are off and allow the entire cooktop to cool to room temperature before resetting. The sensor may need to return to ambient temperature to recalibrate.
3. **Clean the cooktop surface** – Clean the glass surface thoroughly, paying attention to the edges and any areas where spills may have seeped underneath.
4. **Check for glass damage** – Inspect the glass surface for cracks, chips, or damage near the affected zone. Even hairline cracks can affect sensor accuracy.
5. **Note which zone is affected** – If E4 appears with a specific zone number or only when a specific burner is used, this helps a technician identify which sensor has failed.

### When to Call a Professional

The residual heat sensors are located beneath the glass cooktop surface and require professional tools to access and replace. **Contact our Bosch cooktop repair team** for professional sensor replacement using factory-certified parts.

## Error Code E5 – Power Board Communication Error

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/cooktop/e5-power-board-communication-error/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Cooktop Error Code E5 – Communication Error | Causes & Fixes

**Meta Description:** Bosch cooktop showing E5? This indicates a power board communication error. Learn the causes and how to fix the E5 electronic fault on your Bosch cooktop.

**Keywords:** Bosch cooktop error code E5, Bosch cooktop power board communication, Bosch cooktop E5 fix, Bosch cooktop electronic fault, Bosch cooktop control board, Bosch cooktop not responding, Bosch cooktop repair

---

### What Does Bosch Cooktop Error Code E5 Mean?

The E5 error code on a Bosch cooktop indicates a **power board communication error**. Modern Bosch cooktops use a distributed electronic architecture with multiple circuit boards: a user interface (touch control) board that processes your input, and one or more power boards that control the heating elements or induction coils. These boards communicate continuously over a serial data bus.

The E5 error means this communication link has been disrupted — data is not being transmitted correctly between the boards. Without proper communication, the user interface cannot relay your commands to the power board, and the power board cannot report burner status back to the display. The cooktop shuts down to prevent unsafe operation.

This error is found on electronically controlled Bosch cooktops sold in the United States, including both induction and radiant electric models with touch controls.

### Common Causes of the E5 Error Code

Communication between cooktop boards can fail because of:

- **Loose ribbon cable or connector** – The flat ribbon cable or wire harness connecting the boards can work loose due to thermal expansion/contraction cycles during heating and cooling.
- **Heat-damaged ribbon cable** – The communication cable can be degraded by prolonged heat exposure from the heating elements or induction coils below the glass surface.
- **Moisture or spillover damage** – Liquid spills that seep through the cooktop edges can reach the circuit boards and connectors, causing corrosion, short circuits, or signal degradation.
- **Power board component failure** – A failed component on the power board can disable its communication circuit.
- **User interface board failure** – The touch control board can develop faults that prevent communication.
- **Power surge damage** – Electrical surges can damage the sensitive communication circuits on the boards.

### How to Troubleshoot the E5 Error Code

Limited DIY troubleshooting is available:

1. **Power cycle the cooktop** – Turn off the circuit breaker for at least three minutes. This allows all electronic components to fully discharge and reinitialize when power is restored.
2. **Check for moisture** – If a recent spillover or boil-over occurred, liquid may have reached the electronics. Allow the cooktop to dry completely (24-48 hours with the circuit breaker off) before restarting.
3. **Check the touch controls** – After restarting, check if the touch controls respond (display lights up, zones can be selected). If the display is blank or unresponsive, the user interface board may have failed.
4. **Check for stable power** – Ensure the cooktop's circuit breaker is functioning properly and the electrical connections are solid.
5. **Try multiple restarts** – If E5 is intermittent, a thermal expansion issue with a connector may be the cause. Note whether E5 appears only when the cooktop is hot.

### When to Call a Professional

The E5 error requires a technician to inspect the internal connections and test the circuit boards. Accessing cooktop electronics requires removing the cooktop from the countertop. **Contact our Bosch cooktop repair team** for professional electronic diagnosis using factory-certified parts.

## Error Code E7 – Touch Panel Fault

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/cooktop/e7-touch-panel-fault/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Cooktop Error Code E7 – Touch Panel Fault | Causes & Fixes

**Meta Description:** Bosch cooktop displaying E7? This indicates a touch panel fault. Learn the causes and solutions for the E7 control panel error on your Bosch cooktop.

**Keywords:** Bosch cooktop error code E7, Bosch cooktop touch panel fault, Bosch cooktop E7 fix, Bosch cooktop controls not working, Bosch cooktop touch screen, Bosch cooktop buttons not responding, Bosch cooktop repair

---

### What Does Bosch Cooktop Error Code E7 Mean?

The E7 error code on a Bosch cooktop indicates a **touch panel (capacitive control interface) fault**. Most modern Bosch cooktops sold in the United States use capacitive touch controls rather than physical knobs or buttons. These touch controls work by detecting changes in electrical capacitance when your finger touches the glass surface over a sensor pad.

The E7 error means the touch control system has detected a malfunction — the sensors are not responding correctly, multiple sensors are triggering simultaneously without being touched, or the touch controller chip is reporting errors. The cooktop may disable all touch controls when E7 is displayed, preventing any burner operation as a safety measure.

This is a frustrating error because it typically means you cannot control the cooktop at all — you can't turn burners on, adjust power, or access any settings through the touch interface.

### Common Causes of the E7 Error Code

Touch panel faults are most commonly caused by:

- **Liquid or moisture on the control area** – Water, cooking liquid, grease, or condensation on the touch control area changes the capacitance readings and can cause false touches or no response at all. This is the single most common cause of E7.
- **Object resting on the controls** – A pot lid, towel, utensil, or any object placed on the touch control area creates a persistent capacitive input that confuses the controller.
- **Cracked glass over the control area** – A crack in the glass surface above the touch sensors changes the capacitive properties and can cause incorrect or no sensor readings.
- **Failed touch controller chip** – The microchip that processes touch inputs can fail due to power surges, moisture damage, or component aging.
- **Damaged touch sensor pads** – The sensor pads beneath the glass surface can be damaged by excessive heat or moisture intrusion.
- **Spillover damage** – Liquid that seeps through the edges of the cooktop and reaches the touch sensor board can cause short circuits or corrosion on the sensor pads.

### How to Troubleshoot the E7 Error Code

Follow these steps to address the touch panel fault:

1. **Clean and dry the control area** – Wipe the entire touch control area with a clean, dry cloth. Remove all moisture, grease, and food residue. This resolves the majority of E7 errors.
2. **Remove all objects from the cooktop** – Ensure no pots, pans, utensils, towels, or other items are touching or near the touch control area.
3. **Power cycle the cooktop** – Turn off the circuit breaker for 60 seconds. This resets the touch controller and clears any stored error states.
4. **Allow drying time** – If a spill occurred on or near the control area, leave the circuit breaker off for several hours to allow any moisture that may have seeped under the glass to evaporate.
5. **Check for glass damage** – Carefully inspect the glass surface over the touch controls for cracks, chips, or scratches. Even small cracks can disrupt touch sensing.
6. **Test each control** – After reset, try each touch control individually. If specific controls do not work while others do, the sensor pad for those specific controls may be damaged.
7. **Disable the child lock** – Some E7-like symptoms can actually be caused by the child lock feature being engaged. Check your manual for the child lock activation/deactivation method (usually pressing and holding a specific area for 3-5 seconds).

### When to Call a Professional

If E7 persists after thorough cleaning and drying, the touch control board or sensor array may need replacement. **Contact our Bosch cooktop repair team** for touch panel diagnosis and repair using factory-certified Bosch parts.

## Error Code E9 – Safety Shutoff Timer Activated

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/cooktop/e9-safety-shutoff-timer/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Cooktop Error Code E9 – Safety Shutoff Timer | Causes & Fixes

**Meta Description:** Bosch cooktop showing E9? This indicates the automatic safety shutoff timer has activated. Learn why E9 appears and how to reset your Bosch cooktop.

**Keywords:** Bosch cooktop error code E9, Bosch cooktop safety shutoff, Bosch cooktop E9 fix, Bosch cooktop auto shutoff, Bosch cooktop turns off by itself, Bosch cooktop timer, Bosch cooktop repair

---

### What Does Bosch Cooktop Error Code E9 Mean?

The E9 error code on a Bosch cooktop indicates that the **automatic safety shutoff timer has activated and turned off the burner**. This is a built-in safety feature — not a malfunction — that prevents the cooktop from running indefinitely if it has been left on unattended or if the user has forgotten to turn it off.

Bosch cooktops sold in the United States are equipped with an automatic safety shutoff that turns off each burner after a predetermined time period if no user interaction (power level changes, timer adjustments, or other touch control inputs) is detected. The maximum operating time varies by power level:

- **Power levels 1-2:** Approximately 10-12 hours
- **Power levels 3-5:** Approximately 5-6 hours
- **Power levels 6-8:** Approximately 2-3 hours
- **Power level 9 (maximum):** Approximately 1-1.5 hours

These times vary by model but follow the general principle that higher power levels have shorter automatic shutoff times, since the risk of overheating, fire, and damage is greater at higher power.

When the timer expires, the cooktop turns off the affected burner and displays E9 (or a similar indicator) to let you know the shutoff was automatic, not a malfunction.

### Common Causes of the E9 Code

The safety shutoff activates because:

- **Extended cooking without interaction** – Long, slow cooking processes (simmering stock, braising, reducing sauces) at a constant power level can exceed the safety timer. This is the most common trigger.
- **Forgotten burner** – If you leave a burner on and leave the kitchen, the safety timer protects against potential fire or damage.
- **No timer or power adjustments** – The safety timer resets every time you interact with the cooktop controls. If you set a burner and walk away without any further input, the timer counts down from the power-level-specific maximum.

### How to Reset After E9

Resolving E9 is simple:

1. **Simply restart the burner** – The E9 is not an error that needs troubleshooting. Press the power button to turn on the cooktop, select the burner zone, and set your desired power level. The cooktop will resume normal operation.
2. **Interact periodically** – For long cooking sessions, periodically adjust the power level (even just up one level and back down) or use the built-in timer feature. Any touch interaction resets the safety shutoff clock.
3. **Use the timer feature** – Set a timer on the cooktop's built-in clock for your cooking task. On many Bosch models, an active timer prevents the safety shutoff from engaging.
4. **Do not disable the safety feature** – The automatic shutoff cannot and should not be disabled. It is a critical safety feature mandated by appliance safety standards.

### When to Call a Professional

The E9 code does not indicate a malfunction and does not require professional repair. However, if your cooktop turns off much sooner than the expected safety timer duration, or if it turns off and on erratically, there may be a control board or touch panel issue. **Contact our Bosch cooktop repair team** if the shutoff behavior seems abnormal.

## Error Code Er – Child Lock Active

### What Does Bosch Cooktop Error Code Er Mean?

The Er code (sometimes displayed as a key icon, "CL," or "Lo") on a Bosch cooktop indicates that the **child lock (control lock) feature is active**. This is not a fault code — it is a deliberate safety feature that prevents the cooktop from being operated. When the child lock is engaged, all touch controls are disabled except the child lock toggle itself. You cannot turn on burners, adjust power, or access any cooktop functions.

The child lock is designed to prevent children from accidentally turning on the cooktop, but it is also useful for preventing accidental activation during cleaning or when pets might walk across the surface.

This feature is found on all Bosch cooktops with touch controls sold in the United States. Many users accidentally activate the child lock and then believe their cooktop has malfunctioned.

### How to Identify the Child Lock

Depending on your Bosch cooktop model, the child lock indicator may appear as:

- **"Er"** on the display
- A **key icon** or **lock icon** on the display
- **"CL"** (Control Lock) text
- **"Lo"** (Lock) text
- A **red LED** near a key symbol on the control panel

If you see any of these indicators and the cooktop refuses to respond to touch controls, the child lock is almost certainly the cause.

### How to Deactivate the Child Lock

The method varies by Bosch cooktop model, but common approaches include:

1. **Touch and hold the key symbol** – Most Bosch cooktops have a dedicated key or lock symbol on the touch panel. Press and hold this area for 3-5 seconds until you hear a beep and the lock indicator disappears.
2. **Touch and hold the main power button** – On some models, pressing and holding the main on/off button for 4-6 seconds toggles the child lock.
3. **Two-finger touch** – Certain models require touching two specific areas simultaneously (often the lock symbol and the main power button) for 3 seconds.
4. **Slide gesture** – Some newer Bosch cooktops with slider controls require a swipe gesture across the lock area.
5. **Consult your owner's manual** – The exact child lock activation/deactivation method is model-specific. If you've lost your manual, Bosch provides downloadable manuals on their website using your model number.

### Preventing Accidental Activation

To avoid accidentally engaging the child lock:

- **Know the activation gesture** – Familiarize yourself with how the child lock is activated on your specific model so you can recognize when you've accidentally triggered it.
- **Be careful during cleaning** – Wiping the touch controls with a damp cloth can register as touch inputs and accidentally toggle the child lock.
- **Keep the cooktop dry** – Moisture on the touch controls can cause random inputs, including child lock activation.
