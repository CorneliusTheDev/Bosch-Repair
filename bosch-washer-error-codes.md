### What Does Bosch Washer Error Code E02 Mean?

If your Bosch washing machine is displaying the E02 error code, it indicates a **motor or drive system failure**. The drive motor is the primary component responsible for rotating the drum during wash and spin cycles. When the control board detects that the motor is not operating within expected parameters — such as failing to reach the correct speed, drawing excessive current, or not responding at all — it triggers the E02 fault code and halts the cycle.

This error code appears across various Bosch washer models sold in the United States, including the 300 Series, 500 Series, and 800 Series front-load washing machines. When E02 is displayed, your washer may stop mid-cycle, refuse to spin, or make unusual grinding or humming noises as the motor struggles to operate.

The E02 error is a significant fault that usually requires professional diagnosis. Unlike simple error codes that can be resolved with a reset, the E02 typically points to a hardware problem that will not clear on its own.

### Common Causes of the E02 Error Code

Several underlying issues can trigger the E02 motor failure code on your Bosch washer:

- **Worn carbon brushes** – The carbon brushes that make electrical contact with the motor commutator wear down over time. When they become too short, they can no longer maintain consistent contact, causing the motor to run erratically or stop altogether. This is one of the most common causes of E02 on older Bosch washers.
- **Defective motor control module** – The motor control unit (also called the inverter board on newer models) regulates the speed and direction of the motor. A blown component on this board can prevent proper motor operation.
- **Failed drive motor** – The motor itself can fail due to burned windings, seized bearings, or internal short circuits. Motor failure is more common in washers that have been in service for many years or have been consistently overloaded.
- **Wiring harness damage** – The wiring connecting the motor to the control board passes through areas subject to vibration and moisture. Over time, connectors can loosen or wires can chafe against other components.
- **Overloaded drum** – Consistently overloading your Bosch washer puts excessive strain on the motor. While a single overload may just cause the washer to struggle, repeated overloading can lead to permanent motor damage.
- **Faulty tachometer** – The tachometer (speed sensor) mounted on the motor measures rotational speed. If this sensor fails, the control board cannot verify motor speed and may trigger E02 even if the motor is physically running.

### How to Troubleshoot the E02 Error Code

Before scheduling a professional Bosch washer repair, try these basic steps:

1. **Reset the washer** – Unplug the washing machine from the wall outlet or turn off the circuit breaker for at least 60 seconds. Plug it back in and attempt to run a cycle. A temporary electronic glitch may have caused a false E02 reading.
2. **Check for overloading** – Remove some items from the drum if it was heavily loaded. Bosch front-load washers have specific weight limits, and exceeding them can strain the motor system.
3. **Listen for motor sounds** – When you start a cycle, listen carefully. Complete silence suggests a motor that is not receiving power. A humming or buzzing sound indicates the motor is receiving power but cannot rotate, possibly due to a mechanical obstruction.
4. **Inspect for obstructions** – Small items like coins, buttons, or underwire from bras can sometimes work their way between the drum and the tub, physically preventing the drum from spinning.

### When to Call a Professional

The E02 error code on a Bosch washer almost always requires professional repair. The motor, motor control module, and tachometer are all located inside the machine and require partial disassembly to access. Testing these components requires a multimeter and knowledge of the expected electrical values. **Contact our Bosch washer repair team** for expert diagnosis using factory-certified Bosch parts and backed by our service warranty.

### What Does Bosch Washer Error Code E03 Mean?

The E03 error code on a Bosch washing machine indicates a **door lock mechanism failure**. Every Bosch front-load washer uses an electronic door lock (also called a door interlock or door latch assembly) that must engage before the washer can begin a wash cycle. This is a critical safety feature — without a properly locked door, water could leak out and the rapidly spinning drum could cause injury.

When the control board sends a signal to the door lock and does not receive confirmation that the lock has engaged within the expected time window, it triggers the E03 error code. This code appears on Bosch 300 Series, 500 Series, 800 Series, and Bosch Compact washer models available in the United States.

You may notice the E03 code accompanied by the washer refusing to start, making a clicking sound from the door area as it repeatedly attempts to lock, or displaying the error after the door appears to be closed.

### Common Causes of the E03 Error Code

Several issues can prevent the door lock from engaging on your Bosch washing machine:

- **Failed door lock actuator** – The electronic actuator inside the door lock assembly can burn out over time. This is the most common cause of the E03 error, especially on washers that are several years old and have experienced thousands of lock-unlock cycles.
- **Broken door strike** – The plastic strike plate on the washer frame that the door latch hooks into can crack or break, preventing the lock from engaging fully.
- **Warped or misaligned door** – If the washer door has been pulled or pushed forcefully, it can shift out of alignment with the door lock mechanism. Even a slight misalignment can prevent the lock pin from reaching the sensor.
- **Wiring connection issues** – The wires connecting the door lock to the main control board can come loose, especially if the washer has been moved or has experienced heavy vibration due to unbalanced loads.
- **Debris in the door seal** – Foreign objects, detergent buildup, or clothing fibers caught in the door boot seal can prevent the door from closing completely, which in turn prevents the lock from engaging.
- **Control board malfunction** – In rare cases, the main control board may fail to send the proper electrical signal to the door lock, even though the lock itself is functional.

### How to Troubleshoot the E03 Error Code

Try these steps before calling for professional Bosch washer repair:

1. **Check the door closure** – Open and firmly close the washer door, ensuring you hear a solid click. Push on the door gently to confirm it is fully seated against the frame.
2. **Inspect the door seal area** – Look around the rubber door boot seal (the gasket between the door and the tub) for any trapped clothing, debris, or buildup that could prevent full door closure.
3. **Examine the door strike** – Look at the plastic hook or strike plate on the washer frame where the door latch catches. Check for cracks, chips, or misalignment.
4. **Reset the washer** – Unplug the washer for 60 seconds, then plug it back in. This resets the control board and clears any temporary electronic errors.
5. **Listen for the lock attempt** – When you close the door and press Start, listen for a clicking or buzzing sound from the door lock area. If you hear the mechanism trying to engage, the actuator may be weak. If you hear nothing, the lock or its wiring may have failed completely.

### When to Call a Professional

If the E03 error persists after basic troubleshooting, the door lock assembly likely needs replacement. This component is located behind the front panel of the washer and requires partial disassembly to access. **Contact our Bosch washer repair team** for reliable diagnosis and repair using factory-certified parts.

## Error Code E04 – Water Leak Detected

### What Does Bosch Washer Error Code E04 Mean?

The E04 error code on a Bosch washing machine indicates that the **AquaStop flood protection system has detected a water leak**. Bosch front-load washers sold in the United States are equipped with the AquaStop system, which uses a float switch in the base pan of the washer to detect any water that has escaped from the wash tub, hoses, or internal plumbing.

When water accumulates in the base pan and lifts the float switch, the control board immediately stops the wash cycle, shuts off the water inlet valve, and activates the drain pump to remove water from the tub. The E04 code is then displayed to alert you to the leak condition.

This is one of the most important safety features on your Bosch washer, as it prevents significant water damage to your floors, cabinets, and the rooms below. However, the E04 code also means something in the washer's water system has failed and needs attention before you can use the machine again.

### Common Causes of the E04 Error Code

Several components can develop leaks that trigger the E04 AquaStop alert:

- **Damaged door boot seal** – The large rubber gasket that seals the front door opening to the wash tub can develop tears, cracks, or holes over time. Foreign objects like coins, zippers, or underwire can puncture the seal, allowing water to leak down the front of the machine and into the base pan.
- **Loose or cracked tub-to-pump hoses** – Internal hoses connecting the wash tub to the drain pump and circulation pump can loosen at their clamp connections or develop cracks from age and heat cycling.
- **Leaking detergent dispenser** – The detergent drawer housing and the hose connecting it to the tub can develop leaks, especially if excess detergent has built up and corroded the plastic over time.
- **Faulty water inlet valve** – The solenoid-operated water inlet valve can develop internal leaks or fail to shut off completely, causing water to slowly drip into the machine between cycles and eventually fill the base pan.
- **Cracked wash tub** – Although rare, the outer tub can develop hairline cracks, particularly at stress points near the bearing housing or at seam welds.
- **Drain pump seal failure** – The drain pump has an internal seal that can wear out, allowing water to seep past the pump shaft and drip into the base pan.

### How to Troubleshoot the E04 Error Code

Follow these steps when your Bosch washer shows the E04 leak alert:

1. **Turn off the water supply** – Immediately close the hot and cold water supply valves behind the washer to prevent additional water from entering the machine.
2. **Unplug the washer** – Disconnect power for safety before investigating the source of the leak.
3. **Check the floor around the washer** – Look for visible water around the base of the machine. Note where the water appears to be coming from — front, back, or underneath.
4. **Inspect the door seal** – Open the door and carefully examine the entire rubber door boot seal for tears, punctures, or areas where the seal has pulled away from the tub or door frame.
5. **Check hose connections** – If accessible, inspect the fill hoses at the back of the washer for drips at the connection points. Also check the drain hose for any visible damage.
6. **Tilt the washer** – If you can safely tilt the washer back slightly, look for standing water in the base pan. The presence of water confirms the float switch triggered correctly.

### When to Call a Professional

The E04 error requires identifying and repairing the exact source of the leak before the washer can be used again. Internal leaks from hoses, the tub, or the pump require disassembly of the machine. **Contact our Bosch washer repair team** for thorough leak diagnosis and repair with factory-certified Bosch parts.

## Error Code E13 – Drain Time Exceeded

### What Does Bosch Washer Error Code E13 Mean?

The E13 error code on a Bosch washing machine indicates that the **drain cycle has exceeded the maximum allowed time**. When your Bosch washer reaches a point in the cycle where it needs to drain water from the tub, the control board activates the drain pump and monitors the water level sensor. If the water level does not drop to the expected level within the programmed time limit (typically 5-8 minutes depending on the model), the control board determines that drainage is obstructed or the pump is malfunctioning and displays the E13 error.

This error code is common across Bosch front-load washer models sold in the United States. When E13 appears, you will typically find standing water remaining in the wash tub, and the washer will stop the current cycle. Your clothes will be left wet and heavy because the washer cannot proceed to the spin cycle without first draining the water.

The E13 is one of the most frequently encountered Bosch washer error codes, and the good news is that many of its common causes can be resolved without professional help.

### Common Causes of the E13 Error Code

Multiple issues can prevent your Bosch washer from draining within the expected time:

- **Clogged drain pump filter** – Every Bosch front-load washer has a debris filter (also called a coin trap) located behind a small access panel at the bottom front of the machine. This filter catches coins, buttons, hair ties, lint, and other small objects before they reach the drain pump. When this filter becomes heavily clogged, water cannot flow through the pump efficiently.
- **Blocked drain hose** – The drain hose that carries water from the pump to your home's drain pipe can become kinked, crushed, or internally clogged with lint and debris buildup over time.
- **Drain pump failure** – The drain pump motor can wear out, seize, or lose its impeller blades. A weak pump may move some water but not fast enough to satisfy the control board's drain time requirement.
- **Foreign object in the pump** – Small items that bypass the filter — such as broken glass, small screws, or fabric threads — can lodge in the pump impeller and prevent it from spinning freely.
- **Standpipe or drain connection issues** – If the drain hose is inserted too far into the standpipe, or if the home's drain pipe itself is partially clogged, water can siphon back into the washer or fail to flow out fast enough.
- **Excess suds** – Using too much detergent or using non-HE (high-efficiency) detergent in your Bosch washer creates excessive suds that the drain pump struggles to clear efficiently.

### How to Troubleshoot the E13 Error Code

These steps can often resolve the E13 drainage error:

1. **Clean the drain pump filter** – Locate the small access panel at the lower front of your Bosch washer. Place towels and a shallow pan beneath it, then slowly unscrew the filter cap. Water will flow out — this is normal. Remove the filter and clean off all debris, lint, and trapped items. Inspect the pump cavity behind the filter for any additional objects.
2. **Check the drain hose** – Ensure the drain hose is not kinked, crushed, or pushed too far into the standpipe (it should be inserted only 6-8 inches). Disconnect the hose from the drain pipe and check for internal blockages by running water through it.
3. **Use HE detergent** – If you have been using regular detergent, switch to an HE-formulated product. Bosch front-load washers require HE detergent for proper operation and drainage.
4. **Reset the washer** – After cleaning the filter and checking the hose, plug the washer back in and run a Drain & Spin cycle to test whether drainage works properly.

### When to Call a Professional

If the E13 error code persists after cleaning the filter and checking the drain hose, the drain pump may need replacement. **Contact our Bosch washer repair team** for professional diagnosis and repair with factory-certified parts.

## Error Code E17 – Water Fill Time Exceeded

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/washer/e17-water-fill-time-exceeded/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Washer Error Code E17 – Fill Time Exceeded | Causes & Fixes

**Meta Description:** Bosch washer showing E17? The washer is taking too long to fill with water. Learn causes and fixes for the E17 fill error on your Bosch washing machine.

**Keywords:** Bosch washer error code E17, Bosch washing machine not filling, Bosch washer fill time exceeded, Bosch washer E17 fix, Bosch washer no water, Bosch washer water supply, Bosch washer repair

---

### What Does Bosch Washer Error Code E17 Mean?

The E17 error code on a Bosch washing machine means that the **water fill cycle has exceeded the maximum allowed time**. When you start a wash cycle, the control board opens the water inlet valve and monitors the water level sensor to track how quickly the tub is filling. If the water does not reach the required level within the allotted time (usually 5-10 minutes), the control board determines that water supply is insufficient and displays the E17 error.

This error is seen across Bosch 300, 500, and 800 Series front-load washers in the United States. When E17 appears, the wash cycle will not proceed because the washer cannot wash clothes without adequate water. The machine may display the code immediately at the start of a cycle or partway through if a mid-cycle fill is required and cannot be completed.

### Common Causes of the E17 Error Code

Insufficient water flow to your Bosch washer can result from several issues:

- **Closed water supply valves** – The most common and simplest cause. The hot and cold water shut-off valves behind the washer may have been accidentally turned off or not fully opened during installation or maintenance.
- **Kinked or crushed fill hoses** – The braided stainless steel or rubber hoses connecting the water supply valves to the washer can become kinked if the washer is pushed too close to the wall.
- **Clogged inlet screens** – Small mesh screens are located inside the water inlet valve where the fill hoses connect. These screens filter sediment and debris from the water supply. Over time, mineral deposits, rust particles, and sediment can clog these screens and drastically reduce water flow.
- **Faulty water inlet valve** – The solenoid-operated inlet valve can fail electrically (the solenoid burns out) or mechanically (the internal diaphragm becomes stuck). A failed valve will not open to allow water into the machine.
- **Low household water pressure** – Bosch washers require a minimum water pressure of approximately 20 PSI (1.4 bar) to fill properly. If your home's water pressure has dropped due to municipal supply issues, a failing well pump, or simultaneous use of multiple fixtures, the washer may not fill fast enough.
- **AquaStop hose activation** – Bosch washers with the AquaStop fill hose system have a built-in shut-off valve that closes permanently if it detects a leak. If the AquaStop valve has tripped, no water will enter the machine.

### How to Troubleshoot the E17 Error Code

Follow these steps to diagnose the water fill issue:

1. **Check the water supply valves** – Verify that both the hot and cold water shut-off valves behind the washer are fully open. Turn them counterclockwise to open them completely.
2. **Inspect the fill hoses** – Check that the fill hoses are not kinked, crushed, or pinched between the washer and the wall. Pull the washer forward slightly if needed.
3. **Clean the inlet screens** – Turn off the water supply, disconnect the fill hoses from the back of the washer, and inspect the small mesh screens inside the inlet valve ports. Clean or replace clogged screens.
4. **Test water pressure** – Run the kitchen or bathroom faucet nearest to the washer. If water flow is weak or sputtering, you may have a household water pressure issue.
5. **Check the AquaStop hose** – If your Bosch washer has an AquaStop fill hose, look at the indicator window on the hose connector. A red indicator means the safety valve has tripped and the entire hose assembly must be replaced.
6. **Reset the washer** – Unplug for 60 seconds, restore power, and attempt to start a new cycle.

### When to Call a Professional

If the E17 persists after checking supply valves, hoses, and screens, the water inlet valve likely needs replacement. **Contact our Bosch washer repair team** for expert repair using factory-certified Bosch parts.

## Error Code E18 – Drain Pump Failure

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/washer/e18-drain-pump-failure/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Washer Error Code E18 – Drain Pump Failure | Causes & Fixes

**Meta Description:** Bosch washer displaying E18 error? This indicates a drain pump failure. Learn the common causes and how to fix the E18 drainage problem on your Bosch washing machine.

**Keywords:** Bosch washer error code E18, Bosch washing machine drain pump failure, Bosch washer not draining, Bosch washer E18 fix, Bosch washer drain pump replacement, Bosch washer water stuck, Bosch washer repair

### What Does Bosch Washer Error Code E18 Mean?

The E18 error code on a Bosch washing machine indicates a **drain pump failure or critical drainage obstruction**. While the E13 code signals that drainage is simply taking too long, the E18 is a more severe code that indicates the control board has detected either no drainage at all or a complete pump malfunction. This distinction is important — E18 typically points to a more serious problem than E13.

This error is one of the most commonly reported Bosch washer fault codes in the United States. When E18 appears, the washer stops immediately, and you will find the drum full of water with your laundry sitting in it. The door lock will remain engaged to prevent you from opening the door and flooding your laundry area.

The E18 code can appear on all Bosch front-load washer models, including the compact 24-inch models popular in apartments and condominiums, as well as full-size 27-inch models.

### Common Causes of the E18 Error Code

A complete drain failure on your Bosch washer is typically caused by:

- **Severely clogged drain pump filter** – The coin trap filter at the bottom of the washer can become so clogged with lint, coins, hair ties, and debris that virtually no water can pass through. This is the single most common cause of E18.
- **Failed drain pump motor** – The drain pump motor can burn out, especially after prolonged operation against a blockage. You may smell a burning odor from the bottom of the washer if the pump motor has overheated and failed.
- **Impeller damage** – The pump impeller (the fan-like component inside the pump that moves water) can break, crack, or become separated from the motor shaft, rendering the pump unable to move water even though the motor is running.
- **Foreign object jamming the pump** – Items like coins, screws, bra underwires, or broken plastic pieces can wedge between the impeller and the pump housing, completely stopping the impeller from rotating.
- **Frozen drain pump** – If your Bosch washer is located in an unheated space (garage, basement) and temperatures drop below freezing, residual water in the pump can freeze and prevent operation.
- **Wiring failure** – A broken wire or corroded connector between the control board and the drain pump will prevent the pump from receiving power.

### How to Troubleshoot the E18 Error Code

Because the E18 leaves water trapped in the drum, you will need to drain the water manually before most troubleshooting:

1. **Emergency drain procedure** – Locate the access panel at the lower front of your Bosch washer. Place towels and a large, shallow pan beneath it. Pull out the small emergency drain hose (a thin black tube next to the filter cap). Remove the cap from this tube and let water drain slowly into your pan. Empty the pan as needed until no more water comes out.
2. **Clean the drain pump filter** – Once the water is drained via the emergency hose, carefully unscrew the filter cap. Remove all debris, lint, coins, and objects from the filter and the pump cavity behind it.
3. **Check for pump impeller rotation** – With the filter removed, reach into the pump cavity and try to rotate the impeller with your finger. It should spin freely in both directions. If it is stuck or feels gritty, there may be debris or damage inside the pump.
4. **Inspect the drain hose** – Disconnect the drain hose from the standpipe or sink drain and check for blockages.
5. **Run a test cycle** – After clearing any blockages, reinstall the filter, plug in the washer, and run a quick Drain & Spin cycle.

### When to Call a Professional

If the E18 error returns after clearing the filter, the drain pump assembly likely needs replacement. **Contact our Bosch washer repair team** for fast, reliable drain pump repair using factory-certified Bosch parts.

## Error Code E21 – Drum Motor Timeout

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/washer/e21-drum-motor-timeout/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Washer Error Code E21 – Drum Motor Timeout | Causes & Fixes

**Meta Description:** Bosch washer showing E21 error? This indicates the drum motor has timed out. Learn what causes the E21 motor timeout and how to fix your Bosch washing machine.

**Keywords:** Bosch washer error code E21, Bosch washing machine drum motor timeout, Bosch washer drum not turning, Bosch washer E21 fix, Bosch washer motor stalled, Bosch washer repair

---

### What Does Bosch Washer Error Code E21 Mean?

The E21 error code on a Bosch washing machine means that the **drum motor has failed to reach the required speed within the allotted time**. During each phase of a wash cycle, the control board commands the motor to spin at a specific RPM — slow agitation during wash, medium speed during rinse, and high speed during the final spin. If the motor cannot achieve the target speed within the timeout period, the E21 error is triggered.

This error is distinct from the E02 code (general motor failure) because E21 specifically refers to a timeout condition — the motor may be running but too slowly, or it may be starting and then stalling repeatedly. The control board gives the motor a set window of time to reach speed, and E21 means that window expired before the target was met.

Bosch washer models across all series sold in the United States can display this error code. It most commonly appears during the high-speed spin cycle, which places the greatest mechanical demand on the motor.

### Common Causes of the E21 Error Code

The drum motor may fail to reach the required speed for several reasons:

- **Overloaded or unbalanced load** – An excessively heavy load or a load that has bunched to one side of the drum creates an imbalance. The motor struggles to accelerate an unbalanced drum because the vibration triggers the imbalance sensors, which cause the control board to limit motor speed.
- **Worn drive belt** – Bosch washers that use a belt-drive system can experience belt slippage if the belt is stretched, worn, or glazed. A slipping belt prevents the motor from effectively transferring power to the drum.
- **Worn carbon brushes** – On models with a brushed motor, the carbon brushes wear down over time and lose their ability to maintain consistent contact with the commutator, reducing motor torque and speed.
- **Bearing failure** – The main drum bearings support the heavy drum and its contents. When these bearings wear out, they create excessive friction that the motor cannot overcome, especially at high spin speeds. You may hear a loud rumbling or grinding noise during the spin cycle before the E21 appears.
- **Motor control board failure** – The inverter or motor control module may not be supplying the correct voltage or frequency to the motor, limiting its speed capability.
- **Seized drum** – In extreme cases, a completely seized bearing or a foreign object wedged between the drum and tub can physically prevent the drum from rotating at all.

### How to Troubleshoot the E21 Error Code

Try these steps before scheduling professional Bosch washer repair:

1. **Reduce the load** – Remove about one-third of the laundry from the drum. An overloaded washer is the most common cause of E21, and the fix is simply reducing the load to within the washer's capacity.
2. **Redistribute the load** – Open the door and manually spread the clothes evenly around the drum. Heavy items like towels and jeans should be balanced with lighter items.
3. **Spin the drum by hand** – With the washer off and unplugged, open the door and manually rotate the drum. It should spin freely with minimal resistance. If it feels stiff, grinds, or makes noise, the bearings may be worn.
4. **Reset the washer** – Unplug for 60 seconds, then restart with a smaller, balanced load.
5. **Listen during operation** – If the drum spins slowly but fails to reach high speed, listen for belt squealing (indicating a worn belt) or loud rumbling (indicating worn bearings).

### When to Call a Professional

If E21 persists with a small, balanced load, internal components like bearings, belts, or the motor itself likely need attention. **Contact our Bosch washer repair team** for comprehensive diagnosis and repair using factory-certified parts.

## Error Code E23 – AquaStop Leak Sensor Activated

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/washer/e23-aquastop-leak-sensor-activated/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Washer Error Code E23 – AquaStop Sensor Activated | Causes & Fixes

**Meta Description:** Bosch washer showing E23? The AquaStop leak protection sensor has been activated. Learn what causes E23 and how to fix the water leak on your Bosch washing machine.

**Keywords:** Bosch washer error code E23, Bosch washing machine AquaStop activated, Bosch washer leak sensor, Bosch washer E23 fix, Bosch washer water on floor, Bosch washer flood protection, Bosch washer repair

---

### What Does Bosch Washer Error Code E23 Mean?

The E23 error code on a Bosch washing machine indicates that the **AquaStop flood protection system has detected water in the base pan of the machine**. Bosch's AquaStop is a multi-layer leak protection system that includes a double-walled fill hose with a safety shut-off valve, a float switch in the base pan, and automatic drain pump activation when a leak is detected.

When the float switch in the base pan rises due to accumulated water, the control board immediately stops the wash cycle, closes the water inlet valve, and may activate the drain pump to empty the tub. The E23 code then displays to alert you that a leak has occurred somewhere in the washing machine's internal water system.

This code is similar to the E04 code, and on some Bosch washer models sold in the United States, E23 and E04 may be used interchangeably. The important point is the same: water has escaped from the normal water path and reached the base of the machine, and the leak needs to be found and repaired before the washer can be used safely.

### Common Causes of the E23 Error Code

Water can reach the base pan through several pathways:

- **Torn or deteriorated door boot seal** – The large rubber gasket around the door opening is the most common leak source. Small tears, holes from foreign objects, or deterioration of the rubber over years of use can allow wash water to escape with each cycle.
- **Loose internal hose clamps** – The various hoses inside the washer (tub-to-pump, tub-to-dispenser, inlet-to-tub) are secured with spring or worm-gear clamps. Vibration over time can loosen these clamps, creating slow drips that accumulate in the base pan.
- **Detergent dispenser housing leak** – The plastic housing that holds the detergent drawer connects to the tub via a rubber bellows hose. Cracks in the housing or a deteriorated bellows can allow water to leak during the fill portion of the cycle.

### What Does Bosch Washer Error Code E32 Mean?

The E32 error code on a Bosch washing machine indicates that an **unbalanced load has been detected** during the spin cycle. Bosch front-load washers are equipped with sophisticated vibration sensors and accelerometers that monitor drum balance during the spin-up phase. When the control board detects that the load has shifted to one side of the drum, creating dangerous imbalance forces, it reduces the spin speed or stops the spin entirely and displays the E32 error.

This is actually a protective feature. A severely unbalanced drum spinning at high speed (up to 1,400 RPM on some Bosch models) generates powerful centrifugal forces that can damage the drum bearings, suspension springs, shock absorbers, and even the machine's outer cabinet. In extreme cases, an unchecked imbalance can cause the washer to "walk" across the floor or damage nearby cabinetry.

The E32 code is common across all Bosch front-load washer models in the United States and is typically the easiest error code to resolve because it is usually caused by how the laundry was loaded rather than a mechanical fault.

### Common Causes of the E32 Error Code

An unbalanced load condition in your Bosch washer can be caused by:

- **Single heavy item** – Washing a single heavy item like a bath towel, blanket, or pair of jeans by itself creates a load that cannot distribute evenly around the drum.
- **Mixed weight items** – Combining very heavy items with very light items in the same load can cause the heavy items to clump together on one side while the light items are flung to the other.
- **Overloading** – Stuffing the drum too full prevents items from moving freely and redistributing during the spin-up phase.
- **Underloading** – Paradoxically, very small loads (one or two items) can also cause imbalance because there aren't enough items to balance each other out.
- **Tangled items** – Long items like sheets, duvet covers, or pants legs can wrap around each other and form a heavy ball that sits on one side of the drum.
- **Worn shock absorbers** – The shock absorbers (dampers) that support the drum can wear out over time, reducing their ability to absorb vibration. Worn shock absorbers make the drum more sensitive to even slight imbalances that the washer would normally handle without issue.
- **Broken suspension spring** – If one of the suspension springs that holds the drum assembly breaks, the drum hangs unevenly, making balance nearly impossible.
- **Washer not level** – A washer that is not sitting level on the floor (all four feet firmly on the ground) will have a lower imbalance tolerance.

### How to Troubleshoot the E32 Error Code

The E32 is usually easy to fix:

1. **Pause and redistribute** – Open the door and redistribute the laundry evenly around the drum. Break up any tangled clumps of fabric. Close the door and restart the spin cycle.
2. **Add or remove items** – If you are washing a single heavy item, add a few towels to create a more balanced load. If the drum is overstuffed, remove some items.
3. **Avoid washing single heavy items alone** – Always wash heavy items like comforters or blankets with a few towels to help balance the drum.
4. **Check washer leveling** – Place a bubble level on top of the washer and adjust the front feet until the machine is level in both directions. Make sure all four feet are firmly on the floor with no rocking.
5. **Listen for unusual noise** – If the E32 appears even with a well-balanced load, listen for loud banging during spin. This may indicate worn shock absorbers or a broken spring.

### What Does Bosch Washer Error Code E33 Mean?

The E33 error code on a Bosch washing machine indicates **inconsistent or erratic water level sensor readings**. While the E28 code signals a general pressure sensor fault, the E33 is more specific — it means the control board is receiving water level readings that fluctuate rapidly or contradict each other between the pressure sensor and the flow meter.

Modern Bosch washers sold in the United States use two independent methods to monitor water level: the analog pressure sensor (which measures the pressure of air trapped in a tube connected to the tub) and the flow meter (which counts how much water has entered the machine through the inlet valve). The E33 is triggered when these two systems disagree significantly — for example, the flow meter reports that 5 gallons of water have entered the machine, but the pressure sensor indicates the tub is nearly empty, or vice versa.

This dual-check system exists as a safety measure to prevent both flooding (if one sensor fails "high") and dry-run damage (if one sensor fails "low"). The E33 means the system has detected this disagreement and stopped operation until the discrepancy is resolved.

### Common Causes of the E33 Error Code

Inconsistent water level readings can stem from several issues:

- **Partially clogged pressure tube** – A partial blockage in the pressure tube causes delayed or dampened pressure readings that don't correspond to the actual water level changes tracked by the flow meter.
- **Air leak in the pressure system** – A small crack or loose connection in the pressure tube allows air to escape, causing the pressure sensor to read lower than the actual water level while the flow meter correctly tracks the incoming water volume.
- **Faulty flow meter** – The flow meter (typically a turbine-style sensor mounted on the inlet valve outlet) can develop debris on its impeller, causing it to either over-count or under-count the water volume.
- **Suds interference** – Excessive suds can create pressure fluctuations in the pressure tube that don't correspond to actual water level changes, causing the two sensing systems to disagree.
- **Drain siphon effect** – If the drain hose is not installed with a proper high loop, water can slowly siphon out of the tub during fill. The flow meter tracks water coming in, but the pressure sensor shows the level isn't rising as expected because water is leaving through the drain.
- **Intermittent pressure sensor** – A pressure sensor that is beginning to fail may give correct readings sometimes and incorrect readings at other times, creating the inconsistency the E33 detects.

### How to Troubleshoot the E33 Error Code

Try these steps to address inconsistent water level readings:

1. **Use HE detergent and measure carefully** – Excessive suds are a surprisingly common cause of water level sensor disagreements. Switch to HE detergent and use the manufacturer's recommended amount — which is typically much less than you might expect.
2. **Run empty rinse cycles** – If suds buildup is suspected, run two to three empty cycles with no detergent on the hottest setting to clear residual detergent from the system.
3. **Check the drain hose installation** – Verify that the drain hose makes a high loop (rising to at least the height of the top of the washer tub) before descending to the drain connection. Without this high loop, water can siphon out during fill.
4. **Reset the washer** – Unplug for two minutes to allow both the pressure sensor and flow meter systems to fully reset and recalibrate.
5. **Run a diagnostic cycle** – Some Bosch washers have a built-in diagnostic mode. Consult your owner's manual for instructions on accessing diagnostics, which can help pinpoint whether the pressure sensor or flow meter is providing inaccurate readings.

### Common Causes of the E42 Error Code

### What Does Bosch Washer Error Code F16 Mean?

The F16 error code on a Bosch washing machine means the **door is not closed properly or the door switch is not detecting closure**. This is one of the most straightforward error codes on a Bosch washer and one of the most commonly encountered. The F-series error codes are used on certain Bosch washer model lines (particularly Bosch/Siemens combined platforms) and function identically to the E-series codes found on other models.

This is a fundamental safety requirement — a front-load washer must never operate with the door open, as the rotating drum and pressurized water would create serious safety hazards.

The F16 code appears on Bosch front-load washers sold in the United States and is particularly common on the compact 24-inch models and the 300 Series line.

### Common Causes of the F16 Error Code

The F16 door error is typically caused by:

- **Door not fully closed** – The most obvious and most common cause. The door may appear closed but hasn't engaged the latch completely. A gentle push usually resolves this.
- **Clothing caught in the door seal** – A piece of fabric, sock, or clothing tag caught between the door glass and the rubber door boot seal can prevent the door from closing completely, even though it may look closed from the outside.
- **Faulty door switch** – The electrical micro-switch that detects door closure can fail after years of use. The switch contacts can wear out, corrode, or stick in the "open" position permanently.
- **Broken door handle or latch** – The plastic door handle or its internal latch mechanism can crack or break, preventing the door from engaging with the lock mechanism on the washer frame.
- **Swollen or displaced door seal** – Over time, the rubber door boot seal can swell, shrink, or shift out of its mounting groove, creating enough interference to prevent full door closure.
- **Door hinge wear** – Worn door hinges can allow the door to sag slightly, misaligning the latch with the strike plate and preventing proper engagement.

### How to Troubleshoot the F16 Error Code

### What Does Bosch Washer Error Code F21 Mean?

The F21 error code on a Bosch washing machine indicates a **motor control fault** — the control system has detected that the drum motor is not responding as commanded. This F-series code is functionally equivalent to the E-series motor error codes and appears on certain Bosch washer model platforms sold in the United States.

When the control board sends instructions to the motor control unit to spin the drum at a specific speed and direction, and the motor either fails to respond, responds incorrectly, or cannot maintain the commanded speed, the F21 error is generated. This code can appear during any phase of the wash cycle — agitation, rinse, or spin — although it is most commonly triggered during the high-speed spin phase when the motor is under the greatest load.

The F21 error will cause the washer to stop mid-cycle with clothes still in the drum. Depending on when in the cycle the error occurs, the drum may still contain water, requiring manual drainage before you can remove your laundry.

### Common Causes of the F21 Error Code

Motor control faults on a Bosch washer can be caused by:

- **Worn carbon brushes** – On models with brushed motors, the carbon brushes that make electrical contact with the motor commutator wear shorter with every use. When they become too short, they cannot maintain reliable contact, causing the motor to run erratically or stall.
- **Motor control board (MCU) failure** – The electronic motor control module that regulates power to the motor can develop component failures — blown capacitors, damaged transistors, or burned traces — that prevent it from driving the motor correctly.
- **Mechanical overload** – A severely overloaded drum, failed bearings, or a foreign object jammed between the drum and tub can create so much mechanical resistance that the motor cannot overcome it.
- **Drive belt issues** – A broken, stretched, or slipped drive belt prevents the motor from transferring power to the drum even though the motor itself may be running.
- **Motor winding failure** – The copper wire windings inside the motor can develop short circuits or open circuits due to overheating, insulation breakdown, or age-related degradation.
- **Blocked drain pump** – In some scenarios, a blocked drain pump can cause the tub to retain water, making the drum heavier than expected and overloading the motor during spin.

### How to Troubleshoot the F21 Error Code

Try these steps before calling for professional repair:

1. **Reset the washer** – Unplug for at least 60 seconds, then power back on. A temporary electronic glitch on the motor control board can sometimes cause a false F21.
2. **Reduce the load** – If the F21 occurred during spin with a heavy load, remove half the items and try again. Motor overload from excessive weight is a common trigger.
3. **Check for mechanical obstructions** – With the washer off and unplugged, open the door and try spinning the drum by hand. It should rotate freely with minimal resistance. If it is stiff, grinds, or won't turn, there may be a mechanical problem (worn bearings, foreign object, broken spider arm).
4. **Clean the drain pump filter** – A clogged filter can prevent water from draining, leaving heavy water in the drum during spin. Clean the filter following the procedure in your owner's manual.
5. **Listen during operation** – If the washer attempts to run after a reset, listen for the motor. Silence means the motor isn't engaging at all. A humming or buzzing suggests the motor is powered but can't turn. Normal motor sounds with no drum movement suggest a belt problem.

### When to Call a Professional

The F21 error usually indicates a hardware problem that requires professional diagnosis — carbon brush replacement, motor control board replacement, belt replacement, or bearing service. **Contact our Bosch washer repair team** for comprehensive motor system diagnosis and repair using factory-certified Bosch parts.

### What Does Bosch Washer Error Code F23 Mean?

The F23 error code on a Bosch washing machine indicates that the **AquaStop flood protection system has been activated**. This is the F-series equivalent of the E23/E04 AquaStop errors found on other Bosch washer model platforms. The AquaStop system is Bosch's proprietary multi-layer leak protection technology, and F23 means it has detected water where water should not be — specifically in the base pan underneath the wash tub.

Bosch's AquaStop system is one of the most comprehensive leak protection systems in the home appliance industry. It operates on three levels: a double-walled fill hose with automatic shut-off, a float switch in the base pan, and software monitoring that compares expected water levels with actual sensor readings. The F23 error is triggered by the float switch, which rises when water accumulates in the base pan.

When F23 activates, the washer immediately stops the current cycle, closes the water inlet valve, and engages the drain pump. This is designed to minimize water damage to your home. The error code is found on Bosch washers sold across the United States and should be taken seriously as it indicates a real water leak.

### Common Causes of the F23 Error Code

The AquaStop system can be triggered by various leak sources:

- **Door boot seal deterioration** – The most common leak source on front-load washers. The large rubber gasket sealing the door opening can develop tears, punctures (from zippers, coins, or underwire), or cracks from age and detergent exposure.
- **Internal hose connection failure** – Multiple rubber hoses inside the washer connect the tub to the pump, the dispenser to the tub, and the inlet valve to the dispenser. Any of these hoses can develop leaks at clamp connections or through material degradation.
- **Drain pump seal wear** – The mechanical seal around the drain pump shaft prevents water from leaking along the shaft. This seal wears over time and can allow a slow but steady drip that accumulates in the base pan.
- **Cracked tub** – The outer plastic tub can develop stress cracks, particularly near the bearing mount if the main bearings have begun to fail and cause excessive vibration.
- **Dispenser housing leak** – The detergent dispenser housing and its connecting hose can crack or loosen, especially if clogged with hardened detergent buildup.
- **Overfill condition** – If the water inlet valve leaks or the pressure sensor malfunctions, the tub can overfill, with water eventually finding its way past the door seal or through overflow paths into the base pan.

### How to Troubleshoot the F23 Error Code

Safety first — you are dealing with a water leak:

1. **Shut off water supply** – Close both hot and cold water valves behind the washer immediately.
2. **Unplug the washer** – Disconnect power for safety before investigation.
3. **Mop up visible water** – Clean any water around the washer to prevent floor damage and slip hazards.
4. **Check the door seal** – Open the door and carefully inspect the entire rubber door boot seal for tears, holes, or areas where it has pulled away from the tub or door frame. Pay special attention to the bottom of the seal where water pools during operation.
5. **Check external connections** – Inspect the fill hoses and drain hose at the back of the washer for drips, loose connections, or visible damage.
6. **Look under the washer** – If you can safely tilt or slide the washer, look for water trails that indicate where the leak originates. Water stains or mineral trails on the base pan can help identify the leak path.
7. **Allow drying time** – After locating and addressing the visible issue, let the base pan dry for several hours before resetting. The float switch will not reset while water remains in the pan.

### What Does Bosch Washer Error Code F43 Mean?

The F43 error code on a Bosch washing machine indicates that the **motor is locked and unable to rotate the drum**. This is one of the most serious motor-related error codes, as it means the control board has attempted to drive the motor but detected zero rotation despite applying power. The motor is physically prevented from turning, or the motor itself has completely seized.

The control board knows the motor isn't turning because the tachometer (speed sensor) on the motor reports zero RPM while the motor control board is supplying power. After multiple failed attempts to start the motor, the control board gives up and displays the F43 code.

This error code appears on Bosch washer models sold in the United States that use the F-series error code numbering system. It is a critical error that prevents the washer from operating in any cycle mode.

### Common Causes of the F43 Error Code

A completely locked motor condition can be caused by:

- **Seized drum bearings** – The main drum bearings can seize completely due to prolonged wear, rust, or loss of lubrication. When bearings seize, the drum physically cannot rotate regardless of how much power the motor applies.
- **Foreign object between drum and tub** – Items like coins, screws, bra underwires, or small toys can fall through the drum perforations and lodge in the narrow gap between the inner drum and the outer tub. If the object wedges tightly, it can lock the drum in place.
- **Broken spider arm** – The spider arm (also called the drum shaft or drum hub) connects the drive shaft to the inner drum. If one or more arms of this component break (often due to corrosion on aluminum spiders), the drum can shift and jam against the tub.
- **Seized motor** – The motor itself can seize due to burned bearings, a shorted winding that has welded the rotor to the stator, or physical damage from impact.
- **Shattered belt tensioner or idler** – If the belt tensioner or idler pulley seizes, it can lock the belt and prevent both the motor and drum from turning.
- **Frozen motor** – In unheated installation spaces, residual moisture in the motor can freeze during cold weather, temporarily locking the motor shaft.
- **Child safety lock activation** – On rare occasions, certain washer models can display F43-like symptoms when a child lock or control lock feature has been accidentally activated, though this would not be a true mechanical lock.

### How to Troubleshoot the F43 Error Code

The F43 is a serious error with limited DIY solutions, but try these basic checks:

1. **Spin the drum manually** – With the washer unplugged, open the door and try to rotate the drum by hand. If it won't move at all, something is mechanically locked. If it moves freely, the problem may be electrical rather than mechanical.
2. **Listen for foreign objects** – Slowly rotate the drum by hand and listen for scraping, clicking, or rattling sounds that would indicate a foreign object trapped between the drum and tub.
3. **Check for the child lock** – Look at the control panel for a key icon or lock icon that indicates the child lock feature is active. Consult your manual for the button combination to deactivate it.
4. **Allow frozen components to thaw** – If your washer is in an unheated garage and it is below freezing, move a space heater near the washer (safely) and allow it to warm up for several hours before trying again.
5. **Reset the washer** – Unplug for 60 seconds and retry. In very rare cases, a software hang can cause the motor controller to not release the motor, and a full power cycle clears this.

### When to Call a Professional

The F43 error almost always requires professional repair. Seized bearings, foreign object removal, and spider arm replacement all require significant disassembly. **Contact our Bosch washer repair team** for expert diagnosis and repair using factory-certified Bosch parts.

### What Does Bosch Washer Error Code F57 Mean?

The F57 error code on a Bosch washing machine indicates that the **voltage supply to the washer is too low for proper operation**. Bosch front-load washers sold in the United States are designed for standard 120V AC household power, with an acceptable operating range typically between 110V and 127V. When the control board's voltage monitoring circuit detects that the supply voltage has dropped below the minimum threshold, it triggers the F57 error to prevent damage to the motor and electronic components.

Low voltage is problematic for washing machines because the motor needs adequate voltage to generate the torque required for drum rotation, especially during the high-speed spin cycle. Running a motor at low voltage causes it to draw excessive current, which generates heat and can lead to premature failure of the motor, control board, and wiring.

The F57 error may appear at the start of a cycle, during the high-speed spin phase (when electrical demand is highest), or intermittently depending on the voltage fluctuations at your home.

### Common Causes of the F57 Error Code

Low voltage reaching your Bosch washer can have several causes:

- **Underrated electrical circuit** – Your Bosch washer should be on a dedicated 20-amp circuit. If the washer shares a circuit with other appliances (refrigerator, microwave, dryer), the combined load can drop voltage below acceptable levels.
- **Long wiring run** – Voltage drops naturally over long wire runs. If the electrical panel is far from the laundry area, the wire gauge may not be sufficient to deliver adequate voltage at the end of the run, especially under load.
- **Corroded or loose connections** – Corroded wire connections at the outlet, junction box, or electrical panel create resistance that reduces the voltage reaching the washer.
- **Extension cord use** – Using an extension cord (especially a long or undersized one) to power a washing machine is a common cause of voltage drop. Bosch specifically recommends against using extension cords with their washers.
- **Utility voltage issues** – Your local power company may be delivering voltage below normal levels during periods of high demand (hot summer afternoons when air conditioners are running, for example).
- **Failing outlet** – The wall outlet itself can develop loose or corroded internal contacts that reduce voltage under load.
- **Shared neutral issue** – In older homes, a shared or damaged neutral wire in the circuit can cause voltage fluctuations.

### How to Troubleshoot the F57 Error Code

Follow these steps to investigate low voltage:

1. **Stop using extension cords** – If your Bosch washer is plugged into an extension cord, plug it directly into the wall outlet. Extension cords are never recommended for washing machines.
2. **Check for shared circuits** – Identify what else is on the same circuit as your washer. Unplug or turn off all other devices on that circuit and see if the F57 error clears.
3. **Test the outlet voltage** – If you have a multimeter, measure the voltage at the outlet with the washer plugged in and running. A reading below 110V confirms a voltage problem. Also compare voltage with and without the washer running — a large drop (more than 5V) indicates a circuit issue.
4. **Check outlet connections** – If you are comfortable with basic electrical work, turn off the circuit breaker and check the outlet for loose or corroded wire connections.
5. **Reset the washer** – If the voltage issue was temporary (a brief utility brownout), unplugging for 60 seconds and restarting may clear the error.
6. **Try a different time** – If the error occurs during peak electricity hours, try running the washer early morning or late evening when grid demand is lower.

### When to Call a Professional

If voltage issues persist, you need a licensed electrician to evaluate your home's wiring. The washer may need a dedicated 20-amp circuit run from the panel. For washer-specific issues, **contact our Bosch washer repair team** if the F57 persists even with confirmed good voltage, as the voltage sensing circuit on the control board may be faulty.
