## Error Code E01 – Freezer Temperature Sensor Fault

**Keywords:** Bosch freezer error code E01, Bosch freezer temperature sensor fault, Bosch freezer E01 fix, Bosch freezer thermistor, Bosch freezer not freezing, Bosch upright freezer repair, Bosch freezer repair

### What Does Bosch Freezer Error Code E01 Mean?

The E01 error code on a Bosch freezer indicates a **temperature sensor (NTC thermistor) fault**. The temperature sensor is a small electronic probe that continuously measures the air temperature inside the freezer compartment and reports this data to the main control board. The control board uses this reading to determine when to run the compressor, when to activate the defrost heater, and when to trigger temperature alarms.

When the control board detects that the temperature sensor is returning readings that are outside the physically possible range — either showing an open circuit (maximum resistance, interpreted as an impossibly cold temperature) or a short circuit (minimum resistance, interpreted as an impossibly hot temperature) — it triggers the E01 error code. Without accurate temperature data, the freezer cannot safely manage its cooling cycle, and food preservation may be compromised.

This error code appears on Bosch upright freezers, drawer freezers, and combination refrigerator-freezers sold in the United States when the freezer section's temperature sensor malfunctions. On combination units, E01 in the freezer section is distinct from the E01 error in the refrigerator section and refers specifically to the freezer compartment sensor.

### Common Causes of the E01 Error Code

The freezer temperature sensor can fail for several reasons:

- **Thermistor component failure** – NTC thermistors experience thermal fatigue from the extreme temperature cycles in a freezer environment — temperatures can swing from 0°F during normal operation to 50°F+ during defrost cycles. This repeated thermal stress gradually causes the sensor's resistance characteristics to drift until they fall outside the control board's acceptable range.
- **Ice encasement** – If the freezer's automatic defrost system is not functioning properly, frost and ice can build up and completely encase the temperature sensor. When ice surrounds the sensor, it is thermally isolated from the air, causing it to read the temperature of the ice rather than the air temperature.
- **Wiring connection looseness** – The connector linking the sensor to the control board can work loose due to vibration from the compressor, or it may not have been fully seated during manufacturing or a previous service.
- **Moisture damage to the connector** – During each defrost cycle, water is produced as frost melts off the evaporator. If the defrost drain is blocked, this water can overflow and contact the sensor's electrical connector, causing corrosion.
- **Physical sensor damage** – The thin probe can be bent or damaged if heavy items are placed against the back wall of the freezer, or if shelves are forced in during restocking.
- **Control board input failure** – The analog input circuit on the control board that reads the thermistor resistance can develop faults, causing the board to misinterpret a healthy sensor's readings.

### How to Troubleshoot the E01 Error Code

Try these steps before calling for professional repair:

1. **Reset the freezer** – Unplug the freezer or turn off the circuit breaker for at least five minutes. This extended reset gives the control board time to fully reinitialize. When power is restored, the board will re-read the sensor from a fresh starting state, which can clear temporary faults.
2. **Check for excessive frost or ice** – Open the freezer and inspect for heavy frost accumulation, particularly on the back wall where the evaporator and sensor are typically located. If the back wall is heavily frosted or iced, the defrost system may be malfunctioning and the sensor may be encased in ice.
3. **Perform a manual defrost** – If frost is heavy, unplug the freezer and leave the door open for 6-12 hours to allow complete defrosting. Place towels on the floor to absorb meltwater. After defrosting, plug back in and monitor whether E01 returns.
4. **Monitor the freezer temperature** – After reset, place an external thermometer in the freezer and monitor the temperature over 24 hours. If the freezer maintains proper temperature (at or below 0°F / -18°C) without E01 returning, the error may have been a temporary glitch.
5. **Check the defrost drain** – While the freezer is defrosted, check that the drain hole at the bottom of the freezer compartment is clear. Flush with warm water to ensure it is not blocked by ice or debris.

### When to Call a Professional

If E01 returns after reset and manual defrost, the temperature sensor or its wiring needs professional testing and replacement. **Contact our Bosch freezer repair team** for expert sensor diagnosis and replacement using factory-certified Bosch parts.

## Error Code E02 – Defrost System Fault

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/freezer/e02-defrost-system-fault/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Freezer Error Code E02 – Defrost System Fault | Causes & Fixes

**Meta Description:** Bosch freezer displaying E02? This indicates a defrost system malfunction. Learn what causes E02 and how to fix the defrost problem on your Bosch freezer.

**Keywords:** Bosch freezer error code E02, Bosch freezer defrost fault, Bosch freezer E02 fix, Bosch freezer icing up, Bosch freezer defrost heater, Bosch freezer frost buildup, Bosch freezer repair

---

### What Does Bosch Freezer Error Code E02 Mean?

The E02 error code on a Bosch freezer indicates a **defrost system malfunction**. Bosch frost-free freezers use an automatic defrost system that activates at regular intervals (typically every 8-12 hours) to melt the frost that naturally accumulates on the evaporator coils. This keeps the evaporator clear for efficient heat exchange and prevents the heavy frost buildup that would eventually insulate the coils and cause the freezer to lose cooling capacity.

The E02 error appears when the control board detects that the defrost system is not performing as expected — either the defrost heater is not activating, the defrost temperature is not rising as expected during the defrost cycle, the defrost is taking too long, or the defrost temperature sensor (used specifically to monitor the evaporator during defrost) is reporting abnormal values.

When E02 is active, the freezer will continue to cool (the compressor keeps running), but without defrost cycles, frost will gradually build up on the evaporator coils over several days. As the frost thickens, cooling efficiency will decrease and eventually the freezer will fail to maintain safe temperatures — a serious food safety concern.

### Common Causes of the E02 Error Code

Defrost system faults can result from failures in several components:

- **Burned-out defrost heater** – The defrost heater is a resistive element (glass tube, wire-wound, or aluminum sheath type) positioned near the evaporator coils. When it burns through and develops an open circuit, the defrost cycle cannot generate heat to melt frost. This is the most common cause of E02.
- **Failed defrost thermostat (bi-metal)** – The defrost termination thermostat cuts power to the heater when the evaporator temperature rises above approximately 50°F (10°C), preventing the heater from running indefinitely. If this thermostat fails in the open position, it permanently breaks the heater circuit.
- **Defrost timer or control board scheduling failure** – On models with a mechanical timer, the timer can stick and fail to advance to the defrost phase. On electronic models, a control board malfunction can prevent the defrost cycle from initiating.
- **Defrost thermistor failure** – Some Bosch freezers use a dedicated NTC thermistor to monitor evaporator temperature during defrost. If this sensor fails, the control board cannot manage the defrost cycle properly and may trigger E02.
- **Wiring damage** – The wiring to the defrost heater, thermostat, and thermistor runs through the freezer's interior and is subject to repeated freezing, thawing, and potential ice damage.
- **Blocked defrost drain** – While not a failure of the defrost heating components, a blocked drain causes meltwater from defrost to refreeze inside the freezer, creating the appearance of a defrost system failure.

### How to Troubleshoot the E02 Error Code

Follow these steps:

1. **Check for frost buildup** – Open the freezer and look for unusually heavy frost on the back wall or ceiling. On most Bosch freezers, you can see the vents through which cooled air from the evaporator circulates. Heavy frost blocking these vents confirms the defrost system is not working.
2. **Manual defrost** – Unplug the freezer, remove all food (place it in coolers with ice), and leave the door open for 12-24 hours until completely defrosted. Place towels to catch meltwater. After complete defrost, plug back in and monitor.
3. **Clear the defrost drain** – While the unit is defrosted, locate and clear the drain hole at the bottom of the freezer compartment. Flush with warm water.
4. **Reset and monitor** – After manual defrost and restart, monitor over 3-5 days. If the back wall frosts over again within a few days (instead of the normal several weeks), the defrost heater or thermostat has failed.
5. **Listen for defrost** – During normal operation, listen for a subtle change in the freezer's sounds every 8-12 hours — the compressor stops and there may be a faint ticking or hissing as defrost activates. If the compressor never pauses and runs continuously, defrost may not be cycling.

### When to Call a Professional

Replacing the defrost heater and thermostat requires removing the interior back wall panel of the freezer to access the evaporator. **Contact our Bosch freezer repair team** for professional defrost system diagnosis and repair using factory-certified Bosch parts.

## Error Code E03 – Evaporator Fan Motor Failure

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/freezer/e03-evaporator-fan-motor-failure/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Freezer Error Code E03 – Evaporator Fan Failure | Causes & Fixes

**Meta Description:** Bosch freezer showing E03? This indicates an evaporator fan motor has failed. Learn the causes and how to fix the E03 fan error on your Bosch freezer.

**Keywords:** Bosch freezer error code E03, Bosch freezer evaporator fan failure, Bosch freezer E03 fix, Bosch freezer fan not working, Bosch freezer not freezing evenly, Bosch freezer warm spots, Bosch freezer repair

---

### What Does Bosch Freezer Error Code E03 Mean?

The E03 error code on a Bosch freezer indicates an **evaporator fan motor failure**. The evaporator fan is the component responsible for circulating cold air from the evaporator coils throughout the entire freezer compartment. It is what turns a passive cold coil into an active air-cooling system that maintains even temperatures throughout the storage space.

Without the evaporator fan, cold air remains trapped in the evaporator section (usually behind the back wall or ceiling panel) while the rest of the freezer compartment gradually warms. The compressor may continue running and the evaporator coils may continue to get cold, but without the fan to distribute the cold air, food temperatures rise and food safety is compromised.

The control board detects E03 when it commands the evaporator fan to run and either detects no current draw (fan not running) or detects that the fan speed is below the expected minimum (using a tachometer or pulse count from the fan's built-in Hall effect sensor on brushless DC fan models).

This error is found on Bosch upright freezers, chest freezers with circulation fans, and drawer freezers sold in the United States.

### Common Causes of the E03 Error Code

The evaporator fan can fail for several reasons specific to the freezer environment:

- **Ice-locked fan blade** – This is the most common cause of E03 on Bosch freezers. If the automatic defrost system is not working properly (or if the freezer door has been left open), ice can form on and around the fan blade and housing, physically preventing rotation. The fan motor may still be functional, but it cannot turn against the mechanical resistance of the ice.
- **Motor bearing failure** – The evaporator fan motor operates in a very cold, humid environment — conditions that are particularly hard on motor bearings. The lubricant in the bearings can solidify at low temperatures, and condensation can cause corrosion. Over time, the bearings seize, preventing fan rotation.
- **Burned-out motor windings** – If the fan has been running against a partial obstruction for an extended period (slightly iced but still partially turning), the motor windings can overheat and burn out.
- **Wiring or connector failure** – The wires connecting the fan motor to the control board can become brittle from repeated temperature cycling, potentially developing cracks in the insulation or breaks in the conductor.
- **Control board fan circuit failure** – The relay or transistor on the control board that powers the evaporator fan can fail.
- **Fan blade damage** – A cracked, broken, or heavily ice-encrusted fan blade creates imbalance and increased load that can stall the motor.

### How to Troubleshoot the E03 Error Code

Follow these steps to address the fan failure:

1. **Listen for the fan** – Open the freezer door and press the door switch (to simulate a closed door) while listening for the fan. You may need to find the door switch (usually a small button or magnetic switch) and press it with your finger. The fan should run when the switch indicates the door is closed. Absence of fan noise is significant.
2. **Check for ice at the fan** – Open the freezer and look for visible ice accumulation on the back wall vents or at the ceiling where the fan is typically located. Heavy icing confirms the fan is ice-locked.
3. **Perform a manual defrost** – Unplug the freezer and allow it to defrost completely (12-24 hours with the door open). This will melt any ice that has locked the fan. Place towels to catch meltwater.
4. **Test after defrost** – After complete defrosting, plug the freezer back in. If the fan runs normally, the problem was ice-locked blades. The underlying cause (defrost system failure) should also be investigated to prevent recurrence.
5. **Reset the freezer** – Even without ice buildup, unplug for five minutes and restore power. A reset can clear temporary electronic faults.

### When to Call a Professional

If the fan does not run after defrosting and resetting, the motor has failed and needs replacement. The evaporator fan is located behind the interior back panel of the freezer. **Contact our Bosch freezer repair team** for professional fan diagnosis and replacement using factory-certified parts.

## Error Code E04 – Compressor Failure or Start Fault

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/freezer/e04-compressor-failure-start-fault/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Freezer Error Code E04 – Compressor Failure | Causes & Fixes

**Meta Description:** Bosch freezer displaying E04? This indicates a compressor failure or start fault. Learn the causes and how to fix the E04 compressor error on your Bosch freezer.

**Keywords:** Bosch freezer error code E04, Bosch freezer compressor failure, Bosch freezer E04 fix, Bosch freezer compressor not starting, Bosch freezer not cooling, Bosch freezer warm, Bosch freezer repair

---

### What Does Bosch Freezer Error Code E04 Mean?

The E04 error code on a Bosch freezer indicates a **compressor failure or compressor start fault**. The compressor is the mechanical pump at the heart of the refrigeration system — it compresses refrigerant gas and drives it through the condenser and evaporator to remove heat from inside the freezer. Without a functioning compressor, the freezer cannot cool at all, and the contents will reach room temperature.

The E04 error is triggered when the control board attempts to start the compressor and either detects no motor current (compressor not starting), detects the compressor drawing excessive current (overloaded or starting problem), or receives a signal from the compressor's thermal overload protector indicating the compressor has shut itself off due to overheating.

This is one of the most serious error codes a Bosch freezer can display. When E04 is active, your food is at risk of spoiling, and immediate action is needed to move food to another freezer or add dry ice.

### Common Causes of the E04 Error Code

Compressor start failures and compressor faults can be caused by:

- **Failed start relay** – The compressor start relay provides the brief electrical boost needed to get the compressor motor spinning. It is a small, inexpensive component plugged into the compressor body. When it fails, the compressor clicks repeatedly without starting. Shaking a failed relay produces a rattling sound from a broken conductor inside.
- **Failed run capacitor** – Some Bosch freezer compressors use a run capacitor to improve motor efficiency. A failed capacitor can prevent starting or cause the compressor to run hot and trip its overload protector.
- **Compressor thermal overload protector tripping** – The thermal overload on the compressor cuts power if the compressor exceeds a safe temperature. Causes include dirty condenser coils, poor ventilation, high ambient temperatures, or a compressor with internal wear that generates excessive heat.
- **Dirty condenser coils** – Dust-clogged condenser coils prevent heat from dissipating, causing the compressor to work harder and overheat. This is especially common in freezers located in dusty garages or utility rooms.
- **Compressor motor failure** – Internal failure of the compressor's motor windings — an open circuit (won't start), a short circuit (trips breaker), or a ground fault (trips GFCI) — requires compressor replacement.
- **Sealed system refrigerant leak** – Low refrigerant charge causes the compressor to run without building pressure, drawing excessive current trying to compensate, and eventually overheating.
- **Extreme ambient temperature** – Very cold temperatures (below 55°F) can cause the compressor oil to become too thick to lubricate properly on startup. Freezers in cold garages may struggle to start in winter.

### How to Troubleshoot the E04 Error Code

These basic steps may help:

1. **Listen for clicking** – Stand near the freezer and listen. A repeating click-click-click pattern every 2-5 minutes is the compressor trying to start and immediately tripping the overload protector. This sound confirms the compressor is receiving power but cannot run.
2. **Clean the condenser coils** – Locate the condenser coils (usually at the back or bottom of the freezer) and vacuum them with a brush attachment. This is the simplest and most effective step to address compressor overheating.
3. **Check ventilation** – Ensure the freezer has adequate clearance on all sides for airflow. Most Bosch freezers require at least 2-4 inches of clearance at the back and sides.
4. **Let the compressor cool** – Unplug the freezer for 20-30 minutes to let the compressor and its thermal overload protector cool completely. Plug back in and listen for the compressor starting.
5. **Check ambient temperature** – If the freezer is in a cold garage (below 55°F), try temporarily moving it to a warmer location to rule out cold-related starting issues.
6. **Protect your food** – If E04 persists and the freezer is warming up, transfer frozen food to a cooler with dry ice or to another freezer immediately.

### When to Call a Professional

Start relay and capacitor replacement is a simple DIY repair in some cases, but compressor replacement and sealed system work require EPA-certified technicians. **Contact our Bosch freezer repair team** for professional compressor diagnosis and repair.

## Error Code E05 – High Temperature Alarm

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/freezer/e05-high-temperature-alarm/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Freezer Error Code E05 – High Temperature Alarm | Causes & Fixes

**Meta Description:** Bosch freezer showing E05? This indicates a high temperature alarm has been triggered. Learn the causes and how to respond to the E05 temperature alarm on your Bosch freezer.

**Keywords:** Bosch freezer error code E05, Bosch freezer high temperature alarm, Bosch freezer E05 fix, Bosch freezer temperature too warm, Bosch freezer food safety, Bosch freezer alarm, Bosch freezer repair

---

### What Does Bosch Freezer Error Code E05 Mean?

The E05 error code on a Bosch freezer indicates that a **high temperature alarm has been triggered** — the internal temperature of the freezer has risen above the safe threshold for food storage. Bosch freezers monitor internal temperature continuously and trigger the E05 alarm when the compartment temperature exceeds a programmed limit (typically around 14°F / -10°C, though this varies by model).

The E05 alarm is both a fault indicator and a food safety warning. Frozen food should be kept at 0°F (-18°C) or below for long-term safe storage. When temperatures rise significantly above this level, ice crystals in the food begin to melt, texture deteriorates, and if the temperature rises high enough for long enough, bacterial growth can occur in thawed proteins.

The E05 code can appear in two scenarios: first, it may accompany another error code (such as E03 for fan failure or E04 for compressor problems) as a consequence of a mechanical failure that is allowing the freezer to warm up. Second, it may appear on its own as a result of external factors like a prolonged power outage, a door left open, or excessive warm food being added to the freezer.

### Common Causes of the E05 Error Code

The high temperature alarm can be triggered by:

- **Door left open** – The single most common non-mechanical cause. Even a slightly ajar door allows warm room air to enter continuously, raising the internal temperature rapidly.
- **Power outage** – During an extended power outage, the freezer temperature will rise. A well-stocked, properly sealed freezer can maintain safe temperatures for 24-48 hours without power, but the E05 alarm will trigger as temperatures rise.
- **Large amount of warm food added** – Adding a significant quantity of room-temperature food to the freezer at once raises the internal temperature temporarily.
- **Compressor or cooling system fault** – If any component of the cooling system has failed (compressor, fan, defrost system), the freezer will gradually warm up and trigger E05.
- **Damaged or worn door seal** – A door seal (gasket) that no longer forms an airtight closure allows continuous warm air infiltration.
- **Freezer location** – A freezer placed directly in front of a heat vent, in direct sunlight, or in an area that experiences high ambient temperatures will struggle to maintain proper temperatures.
- **Freezer overloaded** – An extremely full freezer with poor air circulation can develop warm spots, though a properly functioning freezer should handle a full load.

### How to Troubleshoot the E05 Error Code

Address the high temperature alarm promptly:

1. **Check the door** – Immediately check that all doors are fully closed. Look for items blocking door closure. Press the door firmly shut.
2. **Check the door seal** – Inspect the gasket all the way around the door for tears, gaps, or areas where it has pulled away from the door or frame.
3. **Assess your food** – Check the condition of the food. If it is still frozen solid or has ice crystals, it is likely safe. If items are fully thawed and at room temperature, do not refreeze meat, poultry, or fish — these items should be discarded or cooked immediately if still below 40°F.
4. **Check for a power outage** – Look at other appliances and the freezer's power indicator. If there was a power outage, restore power and allow the freezer to run to return to proper temperature.
5. **Listen for normal operation** – After ensuring the door is closed and power is on, listen for the compressor running and the fan circulating air. Both should be active to bring the temperature back down.
6. **Acknowledge and reset the alarm** – On most Bosch freezers, pressing any button or a specific alarm button silences the E05 alarm. The alarm indicator may remain until the temperature returns to the safe range.

### When to Call a Professional

If E05 appears along with other error codes (E03, E04), or if the freezer temperature remains elevated despite the door being closed and no external causes being apparent, there is a mechanical cooling system failure. **Contact our Bosch freezer repair team** for professional diagnosis and repair using factory-certified Bosch parts.

## Error Code E07 – Door Open Alarm

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/freezer/e07-door-open-alarm/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Freezer Error Code E07 – Door Open Alarm | Causes & Fixes

**Meta Description:** Bosch freezer showing E07? This indicates the door has been left open. Learn why E07 appears and how to fix the door alarm on your Bosch freezer.

**Keywords:** Bosch freezer error code E07, Bosch freezer door open alarm, Bosch freezer E07 fix, Bosch freezer door ajar, Bosch freezer beeping, Bosch freezer door seal, Bosch freezer repair

---

### What Does Bosch Freezer Error Code E07 Mean?

The E07 error code on a Bosch freezer indicates that the **door has been left open beyond the acceptable time limit** or that the door switch is detecting the door as open when it should be closed. Bosch freezers are equipped with a door alarm that activates when the door remains open for more than approximately 60-120 seconds (depending on model). After this time, the control board sounds an audible alarm and displays E07 to alert you that the door is open and cold air is escaping.

This feature is important for energy efficiency and food preservation. An open freezer door allows warm, humid room air to enter, which raises the internal temperature, forces the compressor to work harder, and causes excessive frost buildup on the evaporator coils as the humid air's moisture freezes.

The E07 is a user alert in most cases — simply closing the door resolves it. However, E07 can also indicate a door seal problem, a door switch failure, or a door alignment issue if it appears even with the door closed.

### Common Causes of the E07 Error Code

The door alarm is triggered for several reasons:

- **Door physically left open** – The most common cause. The door was opened and not fully closed, or it was propped open during loading or unloading.
- **Door not fully latched** – The door may appear closed but the magnetic seal is not fully engaged. This often happens when the door is pushed closed gently rather than firmly, or when a food item is positioned to block the door's full closure.
- **Worn or damaged door gasket** – The magnetic gasket around the freezer door degrades over time. Cracks, tears, or areas where the magnet has weakened allow the door to swing open slightly after being closed. A door that slowly swings open will eventually trigger E07.
- **Freezer not level** – If the freezer is tilted forward (front higher than back), gravity pulls the door open rather than helping it swing closed. Most Bosch freezers are designed to be tilted very slightly backward so gravity assists closure.
- **Faulty door switch** – If the magnetic or mechanical door switch that detects closure fails in the "open" position, E07 will be displayed even with the door firmly closed.
- **Overloaded shelves near the door** – Items stored on the door shelves or shelves near the door that extend too close to the door opening can physically prevent the door from closing fully.

### How to Troubleshoot the E07 Error Code

Follow these steps to resolve the door alarm:

1. **Close the door firmly** – Open the door fully and close it again with a deliberate push. Listen for the magnetic seal to click or feel the resistance of the magnets engaging.
2. **Check for obstructions** – Open the door and look for items that may be preventing full closure — a package that is too tall, a shelf that extends too far, or a drawer that isn't pushed in completely.
3. **Clean the door gasket** – Wipe the gasket with warm, soapy water. Food residue, sticky spills, or ice on the gasket can prevent it from sealing properly.
4. **Test the gasket seal** – Perform the dollar bill test: close the door on a dollar bill so the bill is half inside and half outside. Try to pull the bill out. It should offer firm resistance. If it slides out easily at any point around the door, the gasket is not sealing at that location.
5. **Check freezer leveling** – Place a level on top of the freezer. The front should be equal to or slightly lower than the back (tilted slightly backward) to help the door swing closed naturally. Adjust the front leveling feet accordingly.
6. **Acknowledge the alarm** – Press any button to silence the audible E07 alarm once the door is properly closed.

### When to Call a Professional

If E07 persists with the door firmly closed, the door gasket or door switch may need replacement. A warped door or broken hinge can also cause persistent door open alerts and requires professional assessment. **Contact our Bosch freezer repair team** for door gasket and switch service using factory-certified Bosch parts.
