## Error Code E001 – Oven Temperature Sensor Open Circuit

### What Does Bosch Oven Error Code E001 Mean?

The E001 error code on a Bosch oven or range indicates that the **oven cavity temperature sensor has an open circuit**. The temperature sensor (also called a resistance temperature detector or RTD probe) is a long, thin metal probe that extends into the oven cavity, typically from the top rear wall. This sensor measures the oven's internal temperature and reports it to the control board, which uses this information to regulate the heating elements and maintain the set temperature.

An open circuit means the electrical path through the sensor has been broken — the control board sends current to the sensor but receives no return signal. Without temperature feedback, the oven cannot safely regulate heating, so it shuts down and displays E001.

This error code is found on all Bosch wall oven, slide-in range, and freestanding range models sold in the United States. The E001 will prevent the oven from operating in any cooking mode (bake, broil, convection, self-clean) because all modes depend on temperature monitoring.

### Common Causes of the E001 Error Code

The oven temperature sensor circuit can open for several reasons:

- **Failed RTD sensor** – The platinum resistance wire inside the sensor probe can break due to thermal fatigue after years of heating and cooling cycles. This is the most common cause of E001 on older Bosch ovens.
- **Disconnected sensor connector** – The sensor connects to the oven wiring via a plug connector, usually located at the back of the oven behind the rear panel. This connector can work loose, especially after the oven has been serviced or moved.
- **Damaged sensor wiring** – The wires from the sensor to the control board can be damaged by extreme heat, especially if they route near the broil element or are pinched behind the oven during installation.
- **Corroded connector pins** – Heat and humidity can corrode the connector pins between the sensor and the oven wiring harness, eventually creating an open circuit.
- **Sensor probe physical damage** – The metal probe inside the oven can be bent, cracked, or broken if struck by heavy cookware, oven racks, or during aggressive cleaning.

### How to Troubleshoot the E001 Error Code

Before calling for Bosch oven repair, try these steps:

1. **Reset the oven** – Turn off the circuit breaker that powers the oven for at least 60 seconds. Restore power and check if E001 returns immediately. If it doesn't, try preheating to a moderate temperature and see if the error reappears.
2. **Inspect the sensor probe** – Open the oven door and locate the temperature sensor probe (a thin metal rod, usually 4-6 inches long, extending from the rear wall near the top of the oven). Check for visible damage — bends, cracks, or discoloration.
3. **Check the sensor mounting** – Ensure the sensor is securely mounted to the oven wall. If the mounting screw has loosened, the sensor may have shifted and its connector may have pulled partially loose.
4. **Wiggle test** – With the oven unplugged, gently wiggle the sensor probe. If E001 is intermittent (sometimes appears, sometimes doesn't), the issue is likely a loose connection that makes and breaks contact.

### When to Call a Professional

The E001 error almost always requires sensor replacement. The sensor itself is an inexpensive part, but accessing the connector often requires removing the oven from its cabinet or removing the rear panel. **Contact our Bosch oven repair team** for quick, professional temperature sensor replacement using factory-certified Bosch parts.

## Error Code E002 – Oven Temperature Sensor Short Circuit

### What Does Bosch Oven Error Code E002 Mean?

The E002 error code on a Bosch oven or range indicates that the **oven temperature sensor has a short circuit**. While the E001 code indicates an open circuit (broken connection), E002 is the opposite problem — the sensor's resistance is reading much lower than it should be, indicating that the internal resistance wire is shorting across itself or that the sensor wiring has shorted to ground.

When the control board reads an abnormally low resistance from the temperature sensor, it interprets this as an impossibly high temperature (since NTC and RTD sensors have lower resistance at higher temperatures). To protect the oven and your food from what it believes is a dangerously overheated oven, the control board shuts off the heating elements and displays E002.

This error is found on Bosch ovens and ranges sold in the United States. Like E001, it completely prevents oven operation until the fault is resolved.

### Common Causes of the E002 Error Code

A short circuit in the temperature sensor system can be caused by:

- **Internal sensor failure** – The resistance element inside the RTD probe can develop an internal short, where portions of the resistance wire contact each other, reducing the total resistance to a value much lower than normal.
- **Damaged sensor insulation** – The insulating material separating the sensor wires from the metal probe sheath can break down due to extreme heat exposure (especially after self-clean cycles at 900°F+), creating a path to ground.
- **Pinched or damaged wiring** – If the sensor wiring has been pinched between the oven and cabinet, or if the wire insulation has melted near a heating element, the conductors can short together or to the oven chassis.
- **Moisture contamination** – Water or moisture from spills, steam, or cleaning can enter the sensor connector and create a short circuit path between the pins.
- **Control board sensor input failure** – In rare cases, the input circuit on the control board can develop a component failure that causes it to read an incorrectly low resistance.

### How to Troubleshoot the E002 Error Code

Try these steps before scheduling professional repair:

1. **Reset the oven** – Turn off the circuit breaker for 60 seconds. A temporary moisture-related short can sometimes dry out when the oven is powered off.
2. **Let the oven cool completely** – If E002 appeared during or after a high-temperature cook or self-clean cycle, let the oven cool to room temperature. Heat-related insulation breakdown can sometimes heal as the sensor cools.
3. **Check for moisture** – If the oven was recently cleaned with water or steam, wait 24 hours for complete drying before attempting to use the oven again.
4. **Inspect the sensor** – Look at the temperature probe inside the oven. Check for visible damage, discoloration, or any contact between the sensor wires and the oven wall.
5. **Check after self-clean** – If E002 appeared after a self-clean cycle, the extreme heat (900°F+) may have permanently damaged the sensor insulation. This is common and the sensor will need replacement.

### When to Call a Professional

A shorted temperature sensor needs to be replaced. The sensor's connector and wiring should also be inspected for damage. **Contact our Bosch oven repair team** for professional sensor diagnosis and replacement using factory-certified Bosch parts.

## Error Code E005 – Oven Door Lock Fault

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/oven/e005-door-lock-fault/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Oven Error Code E005 – Door Lock Fault | Causes & Fixes

### What Does Bosch Oven Error Code E005 Mean?

The E005 error code on a Bosch oven indicates a **door lock mechanism fault**. The oven door lock is a motorized or solenoid-driven mechanism that physically locks the oven door during the self-cleaning cycle and during pyrolytic cleaning. At temperatures exceeding 550°F (during self-clean, the oven reaches approximately 900°F), the door must be locked to prevent accidental opening, which would release extreme heat and potentially cause burns.

The E005 error is triggered when the control board commands the door lock to engage (or disengage) and does not receive the expected confirmation signal within the timeout period. This could mean the lock failed to engage before self-clean, failed to disengage after self-clean completed, or is reporting an inconsistent position.

This error appears on Bosch wall ovens and ranges sold in the United States that are equipped with self-cleaning capability. The E005 can prevent the self-clean cycle from starting, interrupt a self-clean cycle in progress, or prevent you from opening the oven door after self-clean is complete.

### Common Causes of the E005 Error Code

The oven door lock can malfunction for several reasons:

- **Worn or failed lock motor** – The small motor that drives the lock mechanism can burn out after many self-clean cycles. This is the most common cause of E005.
- **Broken lock linkage** – The mechanical linkage that connects the lock motor to the actual locking latch can break, bend, or become disconnected, preventing the lock from fully engaging or disengaging.
- **Lock position switch failure** – Micro-switches inside the lock assembly detect whether the lock is in the locked or unlocked position. If these switches fail, the control board cannot confirm the lock's position.
- **Oven too hot to unlock** – After self-clean, the oven must cool below a safe temperature (typically 550°F) before the door lock will disengage. If the oven temperature sensor is reading inaccurately, the control board may not know the oven is cool enough to unlock.
- **Wiring issues** – The wires connecting the lock motor and position switches to the control board can be damaged by the extreme heat generated during self-clean cycles.
- **Food or debris in the latch area** – Baked-on food residue or cleaning product buildup around the door latch can prevent the lock from fully engaging or disengaging.

### How to Troubleshoot the E005 Error Code

Follow these steps to address the door lock fault:

1. **Wait for the oven to cool** – If E005 appeared during or after self-clean, allow the oven to cool completely to room temperature. The lock will not disengage until the oven is below the safe temperature threshold. This can take 1-3 hours after self-clean ends.
2. **Reset the oven** – Turn off the circuit breaker for at least two minutes, then restore power. When the oven powers up, it will attempt to reset the door lock to the unlocked position. You may hear the lock motor running during this process.
3. **Try the self-clean cycle cancel** – If the door is locked and you want to cancel self-clean, press Cancel/Off and wait. The oven will begin cooling, and the lock should disengage once the temperature drops sufficiently.
4. **Clean the latch area** – If the door is unlocked and you can open it, clean any food residue or debris from the door latch hook and the lock mechanism area on the oven frame.
5. **Do not force the lock** – Never attempt to manually force the door open when the lock is engaged. This can break the lock mechanism, the door hinges, or the door itself.

### When to Call a Professional

If the door lock will not unlock after the oven has completely cooled and been reset, or if E005 prevents self-clean from starting, the lock assembly needs replacement. **Contact our Bosch oven repair team** for professional door lock repair using factory-certified Bosch parts.

## Error Code E010 – Oven Overheating

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/oven/e010-oven-overheating/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Oven Error Code E010 – Oven Overheating | Causes & Fixes

**Meta Description:** Bosch oven showing E010? This indicates the oven is overheating. Learn what causes E010 and how to safely fix the overheating problem on your Bosch oven.

**Keywords:** Bosch oven error code E010, Bosch oven overheating, Bosch oven E010 fix, Bosch oven too hot, Bosch oven temperature runaway, Bosch oven safety shutoff, Bosch oven repair

---

### What Does Bosch Oven Error Code E010 Mean?

The E010 error code on a Bosch oven indicates that the **oven has exceeded its safe maximum operating temperature**. This is a critical safety error — the oven has detected a temperature runaway condition where the internal temperature has risen well above the set cooking temperature. The control board immediately cuts power to all heating elements and displays E010 to protect the oven, your kitchen, and your home from potential fire or heat damage.

Bosch ovens sold in the United States are designed with multiple layers of overheating protection. The E010 error is typically the second level of protection — the first being the temperature regulation system that normally cycles the heating elements on and off to maintain the set temperature. When this regulation fails and the temperature continues climbing, the E010 safety threshold is reached.

The E010 is one of the most serious oven error codes and should never be ignored. An overheating oven poses fire and burn risks.

### Common Causes of the E010 Error Code

Oven overheating can be caused by:

- **Relay stuck closed on the control board** – The relay that controls power to the bake or broil element can weld itself in the "on" position, delivering continuous power to the heating element regardless of the control board's commands. This is the most common cause of true oven overheating.
- **Temperature sensor failure** – If the temperature sensor fails in a way that reports a lower temperature than actual, the control board keeps heating the oven because it believes the set temperature hasn't been reached.
- **Cooling fan failure** – Bosch ovens use cooling fans to prevent the oven's electronic components and surrounding cabinetry from overheating. If the cooling fan fails, the control board area can overheat, causing erratic behavior.
- **Convection fan failure** – In convection mode, the fan distributes heat evenly. If the fan fails, hot spots develop near the heating elements and can trigger the overheat sensor locally.
- **Self-clean cycle malfunction** – During self-clean, the oven intentionally reaches very high temperatures (~900°F). If the temperature control fails during self-clean, the oven can exceed even the self-clean temperature limit.
- **Blocked ventilation** – If the oven's ventilation openings (usually at the top behind the control panel or under a rear vent) are blocked, heat cannot escape properly, causing the control area and surrounding cabinet to overheat.

### How to Troubleshoot the E010 Error Code

Safety is the top priority with an overheating oven:

1. **Turn off the oven immediately** – If you haven't already, press Cancel/Off and turn off the circuit breaker. Do not wait for the oven to cool down on its own with power still connected — if a relay is stuck, the element will keep heating.
2. **Open the oven door slightly** – Once power is disconnected, crack the oven door open to allow heat to dissipate. Do not open fully — a rush of extremely hot air can cause burns or damage surrounding surfaces.
3. **Do not use the oven** – Until the E010 is professionally diagnosed and repaired, do not use the oven. A stuck relay or failed control board can cause the oven to overheat again, potentially creating a fire hazard.
4. **Check the cooling fan** – After the oven cools to room temperature, restore power briefly and listen for the cooling fan running. If it doesn't start, the fan motor may have failed.
5. **Check ventilation** – Ensure nothing is blocking the oven's vent openings — no towels, food containers, or storage items placed over the vents.

### When to Call a Professional

The E010 error requires professional diagnosis to determine whether the control board relay, temperature sensor, or cooling fan has failed. Do not continue using an oven that has overheated. **Contact our Bosch oven repair team** for urgent safety diagnosis and repair using factory-certified Bosch parts.

## Error Code E011 – Broil Element Failure

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/oven/e011-broil-element-failure/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Oven Error Code E011 – Broil Element Failure | Causes & Fixes

**Meta Description:** Bosch oven showing E011? This indicates the broil element has failed. Learn what causes the E011 broil error and how to fix your Bosch oven or range.

**Keywords:** Bosch oven error code E011, Bosch oven broil element failure, Bosch oven E011 fix, Bosch oven broiler not working, Bosch oven upper element, Bosch oven not broiling, Bosch oven repair

---

### What Does Bosch Oven Error Code E011 Mean?

The E011 error code on a Bosch oven indicates a **broil (upper) heating element failure**. The broil element is located at the top of the oven cavity and provides direct radiant heat from above. It is used for broiling, browning, and in some oven modes as a secondary heat source during baking and convection cooking.

When the control board activates the broil element circuit and detects that the element is not drawing the expected current — either drawing no current (open circuit) or drawing excessive current (short circuit) — it triggers the E011 error. This prevents the oven from operating in broil mode and may affect other modes that utilize the broil element.

This error appears on Bosch wall oven and range models sold in the United States. On double-oven models, the E011 refers to the broil element in the specific cavity displaying the error.

### Common Causes of the E011 Error Code

The broil element can fail for several reasons:

- **Burned-through element** – The broil element is a resistive heating coil that glows red-hot during operation. Over time, thin spots in the element wire develop and eventually burn through, creating an open circuit. You may see a visible break, burn mark, or hole in the element.
- **Element wire separation** – The element wire can separate from its terminal connection inside the oven due to thermal cycling fatigue.
- **Shorted element** – Less commonly, the element wire can contact the metal sheath or another part of the element, creating a short circuit that trips the breaker or triggers the control board's overcurrent protection.
- **Faulty broil relay on control board** – The relay on the control board that controls power to the broil element can fail, preventing the element from receiving power even though the element itself is functional.
- **Wiring damage** – The high-temperature wiring connecting the broil element to the control board can deteriorate, especially near the element's terminals where temperatures are highest.
- **Self-clean damage** – The extreme temperatures during self-clean cycles (900°F+) accelerate element wear and can cause a weakened element to fail.

### How to Troubleshoot the E011 Error Code

Basic troubleshooting steps:

1. **Visual inspection** – Turn on the broil function and carefully look at the broil element through the oven window (do not stare directly — it should glow red if working). If part of the element does not glow, or if you see a visible break, blister, or burn spot, the element has failed.
2. **Check for sparking** – If you saw sparking, arcing, or a bright flash from the element when you activated broil, the element has shorted and should not be used again until replaced.
3. **Reset the oven** – Turn off the circuit breaker for 60 seconds and restore power. If E011 was caused by a temporary electronic fault, it may clear.
4. **Try bake mode** – If bake mode works normally (the lower element heats), this confirms the issue is specific to the broil element or its circuit.

### When to Call a Professional

Broil element replacement requires disconnecting the oven from power and accessing the element connections, typically from inside the oven cavity or from behind the rear panel. **Contact our Bosch oven repair team** for safe element replacement using factory-certified Bosch parts.

**Keywords:** Bosch oven error code E030, Bosch oven bake element failure, Bosch oven E030 fix, Bosch oven lower element not working, Bosch oven not heating from bottom, Bosch oven repair

---

### What Does Bosch Oven Error Code E030 Mean?

The E030 error code on a Bosch oven indicates a **bake (lower) heating element failure**. The bake element is located at the bottom of the oven cavity and provides the primary heat source for standard baking, roasting, and most cooking modes. It works in conjunction with the convection fan (in convection mode) and the broil element (in some combination bake modes).

When the control board activates the bake element and detects no current draw or abnormal current, it triggers E030. This error prevents the oven from operating in bake mode, though the broil function may still work independently.

The bake element is typically the most-used heating element in the oven, and its failure is one of the most common Bosch oven repair issues reported in the United States.

### Common Causes of the E030 Error Code

The bake element can fail for the same reasons as the broil element:

- **Burned-through element wire** – The most common failure mode. The resistive wire develops a weak spot that eventually burns through, creating a visible break or hole in the element. Often you can see a bright, rough-edged spot on the element surface.
- **Element terminal connection failure** – The bake element connects to the oven wiring at terminals that pass through the rear oven wall. Thermal expansion and contraction can loosen these connections over time.
- **Short circuit in the element** – A short between the element wire and its metal sheath can trip the circuit breaker or blow a fuse on the control board.
- **Control board bake relay failure** – The relay that supplies power to the bake element can fail, cutting power to the element.
- **Hidden element damage** – Some Bosch oven models have a concealed (hidden) bake element beneath the oven floor. On these models, you cannot visually inspect the element without removing the oven floor panel.
- **Post self-clean failure** – Self-clean's extreme temperatures accelerate wear on both the bake and broil elements.

### How to Troubleshoot the E030 Error Code

Try these basic steps:

1. **Visual inspection** – Set the oven to bake at 350°F and watch the bottom element through the oven window. If it does not begin to glow red-orange within 2-3 minutes, it may have failed. If you see a spot that is glowing much brighter than the rest (or sparking), the element is shorting and should be turned off immediately.
2. **Check for a hidden element** – If your Bosch oven has a smooth oven floor with no visible element, the bake element is hidden beneath the floor panel. You cannot visually inspect it without removing this panel.
3. **Reset the oven** – Power off at the circuit breaker for 60 seconds and try again.
4. **Test the broil** – If the broil element works, the power supply and main control board are likely fine, pointing to the bake element or its specific relay.

### When to Call a Professional

Bake element replacement is a common oven repair. Hidden bake elements require removing the oven floor panel. **Contact our Bosch oven repair team** for professional element replacement using factory-certified Bosch parts.

## Error Code E101 – Convection Fan Motor Fault

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/oven/e101-convection-fan-motor-fault/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Oven Error Code E101 – Convection Fan Fault | Causes & Fixes

**Meta Description:** Bosch oven showing E101? This indicates a convection fan motor fault. Learn the causes and solutions for the E101 fan error on your Bosch oven.

**Keywords:** Bosch oven error code E101, Bosch oven convection fan fault, Bosch oven E101 fix, Bosch oven fan not working, Bosch oven uneven heating, Bosch convection oven repair, Bosch oven repair

---

### What Does Bosch Oven Error Code E101 Mean?

The E101 error code on a Bosch oven indicates a **convection fan motor fault**. The convection fan is located behind the rear wall of the oven cavity and circulates hot air throughout the oven for even, consistent cooking. Most Bosch ovens sold in the United States — including wall ovens and slide-in ranges — feature convection cooking as a standard feature, and many include a dedicated convection heating element (called a ring element or circular element) surrounding the fan.

When the control board activates the convection fan and detects that it is not running (no current draw), running too slowly, or drawing excessive current (indicating a mechanical bind), it triggers the E101 error. This error prevents convection cooking modes from operating, though standard bake and broil modes may still function.

### Common Causes of the E101 Error Code

The convection fan motor can fault for several reasons:

- **Fan motor burnout** – The electric motor that drives the convection fan can fail due to worn bearings, overheated windings, or age. The motor operates in an extremely hot environment (inside the oven cavity) and is subjected to significant thermal stress.
- **Seized fan bearing** – The fan motor bearings can dry out and seize in the high-heat oven environment. Before complete seizure, you may hear a loud squealing, scraping, or grinding noise during convection operation.
- **Fan blade obstruction** – Aluminum foil, oven liners, or parchment paper that has blown loose inside the oven can wrap around the fan blade or get sucked into the fan opening, blocking rotation.
- **Disconnected motor wiring** – The wires connecting the fan motor to the control board run through the back of the oven and can be damaged by heat or loosened at their connectors.
- **Control board fan relay failure** – The relay that controls power to the convection fan motor can fail on the control board.
- **Broken fan blade** – A cracked or broken fan blade creates an imbalance that can cause excessive vibration and trigger the motor's thermal protection.

### How to Troubleshoot the E101 Error Code

Try these steps to diagnose the convection fan issue:

1. **Listen for the fan** – Select a convection cooking mode and listen for the fan running behind the rear wall of the oven. Silence means the fan isn't operating. Grinding, squealing, or rattling suggests a mechanical problem.
2. **Check for obstructions** – Open the oven and inspect the rear wall. On most Bosch ovens, you can see the fan through a circular cover or opening. Look for aluminum foil, parchment, or other items that may have been sucked against the fan cover.
3. **Remove oven racks and liners** – Take out all racks, liners, and loose items from the oven. An aluminum foil liner on the oven floor is a common culprit — loose pieces can reach the fan.
4. **Test standard bake** – Select a standard bake mode (non-convection). If the oven heats normally in standard mode, the fan motor or its circuit is specifically at fault.
5. **Reset the oven** – Turn off the circuit breaker for 60 seconds and try a convection mode again.

### When to Call a Professional

The convection fan motor is located behind the rear oven wall and requires removing the oven from its installation to access from behind. **Contact our Bosch oven repair team** for professional fan motor replacement using factory-certified Bosch parts.

## Error Code E115 – Oven Cooling Fan Failure

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/oven/e115-cooling-fan-failure/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Oven Error Code E115 – Cooling Fan Failure | Causes & Fixes

**Meta Description:** Bosch oven showing E115? This indicates the cooling fan has failed. Learn what causes the E115 cooling fan error and how to fix your Bosch oven or range.

**Keywords:** Bosch oven error code E115, Bosch oven cooling fan failure, Bosch oven E115 fix, Bosch oven ventilation fan, Bosch oven cabinet overheating, Bosch oven repair

---

### What Does Bosch Oven Error Code E115 Mean?

The E115 error code on a Bosch oven indicates a **cooling fan failure**. The cooling fan (also called the tangential fan or ventilation fan) is a critical safety component that draws room air across the outer walls of the oven to keep the oven's control panel, electronic components, and surrounding cabinetry at safe temperatures during operation.

Without the cooling fan, the extreme heat generated inside the oven cavity (especially during self-clean cycles) would transfer through the oven walls and potentially damage electronic components, melt plastic parts on the control panel, cause cabinet discoloration or warping, and create a fire hazard with adjacent cabinetry.

When the control board detects that the cooling fan is not operating (through current monitoring or a tachometer signal), it triggers E115 and may shut down the oven to prevent overheating damage. On many Bosch oven models, the cooling fan starts automatically when the oven temperature exceeds a certain threshold and continues running after the oven is turned off until the oven cools down.

### Common Causes of the E115 Error Code

The cooling fan can fail for these reasons:

- **Fan motor burnout** – Like any motor, the cooling fan motor can fail due to worn bearings, overheated windings, or electrical component failure.
- **Blocked fan intake or exhaust** – The cooling fan draws air in from one side and exhausts it from the other (usually out the vent at the top of the oven or behind the control panel). Blocked airflow paths can stall the fan or reduce its effectiveness.
- **Dust and grease accumulation** – The cooling fan operates in an environment where kitchen grease, dust, and cooking vapors are present. Accumulation on the fan blades and motor can slow the fan or seize the motor.
- **Wiring issues** – The wires to the cooling fan can be damaged by heat from the oven walls, especially in built-in installations with tight clearances.
- **Control board fan circuit failure** – The control board circuit that monitors and powers the cooling fan can develop faults.
- **Fan blade damage** – A cracked or broken fan blade creates imbalance and noise, and can trigger the motor's protection circuit.

### How to Troubleshoot the E115 Error Code

Address the cooling fan issue with these steps:

1. **Listen for the cooling fan** – Turn on the oven and set it to bake at 350°F. The cooling fan should start running within a few minutes as the oven warms up. Listen for its sound — typically a soft whirring from behind the control panel or the top vent area.
2. **Check ventilation** – Ensure nothing is blocking the oven vent (at the top, behind the backsplash, or wherever your model vents). Items stored on or near the vent can block airflow.
3. **Clean the vent area** – Vacuum any visible dust or debris from the oven vent opening.
4. **Reset the oven** – Turn off the circuit breaker for 60 seconds and restart. A temporary electronic fault may clear with a reset.
5. **Do not use the oven without the cooling fan** – If the cooling fan is not running, avoid using the oven until it is repaired, as surrounding cabinetry and electronics can be damaged by excessive heat.

### When to Call a Professional

The cooling fan is located inside the oven's housing and requires removal of panels or the entire oven from its cabinet to access. **Contact our Bosch oven repair team** for professional cooling fan replacement.

## Error Code E211 – Door Lock Motor Failure

**SEO Title:** Bosch Oven Error Code E211 – Door Lock Motor Failure | Causes & Fixes

**Meta Description:** Bosch oven displaying E211? This indicates the door lock motor has failed. Learn the causes and how to fix the E211 door lock error on your Bosch oven.

**Keywords:** Bosch oven error code E211, Bosch oven door lock motor failure, Bosch oven E211 fix, Bosch oven self-clean lock, Bosch oven door stuck locked, Bosch oven door lock replacement, Bosch oven repair

---

### What Does Bosch Oven Error Code E211 Mean?

The E211 error code on a Bosch oven indicates a **door lock motor failure**. This is a more specific version of the E005 door lock fault — while E005 covers general door lock issues, E211 pinpoints the failure to the electric motor that physically drives the locking mechanism.

The door lock motor is a small, gear-reduced motor that moves the lock slide from the unlocked to the locked position (and back) in response to commands from the control board. It must engage before self-clean can begin and must disengage after self-clean is complete and the oven has cooled.

When the control board powers the motor and does not receive confirmation that the lock has reached its target position within the expected timeframe, it triggers E211. This could mean the motor is not running at all, is running but cannot move the lock slide (mechanical jam), or the position feedback switches are not reporting correctly.

This error is found on Bosch wall ovens and ranges in the United States that feature self-clean capability.

### Common Causes of the E211 Error Code

The door lock motor can fail for several reasons:

- **Motor burnout** – The lock motor can burn out due to age, repeated self-clean cycles, or overheating from the extreme temperatures during self-clean.
- **Stripped motor gears** – The gear reduction mechanism inside the lock motor assembly can strip its teeth, allowing the motor to spin freely without moving the lock slide.
- **Mechanical jam** – Baked-on food, grease, or corrosion on the lock slide can create enough friction to stall the motor.
- **Lock slide binding** – Thermal warping of the oven frame or lock bracket after many self-clean cycles can cause the lock slide to bind.
- **Position switch failure** – The micro-switches that report lock position can fail, preventing the control board from knowing the lock's actual position.
- **Wiring damage** – The motor wiring, routed near the top of the oven where temperatures are extremely high during self-clean, can deteriorate over time.

### How to Troubleshoot the E211 Error Code

Follow these steps:

1. **Allow cooling** – If E211 appeared during or after self-clean, wait until the oven has completely cooled (2-4 hours).
2. **Reset the oven** – Turn off the circuit breaker for two minutes, then restore power. The oven will attempt to reset the lock to the default (unlocked) position on startup.
3. **Listen for the motor** – When the oven resets, listen near the top of the oven door for the lock motor running (a soft whirring or clicking for several seconds). If you hear nothing, the motor is likely dead.
4. **Clean the lock area** – If the door is unlocked and open, carefully clean any food residue from the door latch and lock mechanism area using a damp cloth.
5. **Do not force the door** – If the door is locked, do not pry it open. The lock mechanism can be damaged, and the door glass can shatter.

### When to Call a Professional

The door lock assembly is located at the top of the oven frame behind the control panel and requires partial disassembly to access. **Contact our Bosch oven repair team** for professional door lock motor replacement using factory-certified Bosch parts.

**Keywords:** Bosch oven error code E301, Bosch oven control board communication failure, Bosch oven E301 fix, Bosch oven electronic fault, Bosch oven display not working, Bosch oven control panel, Bosch oven repair

---

### What Does Bosch Oven Error Code E301 Mean?

The E301 error code on a Bosch oven indicates a **control board communication failure**. Modern Bosch ovens use two main electronic boards: the Electronic Oven Control (EOC) board that manages heating elements, temperature regulation, and oven functions, and the user interface (UI) board behind the control panel that handles the display, touch controls or knob inputs, and user interaction.

These boards communicate over a serial data bus. The E301 error means this communication has broken down — the boards are not exchanging data correctly. This can result in the oven not responding to controls, displaying incorrect information, or refusing to start any cooking mode.

This error is found on electronically controlled Bosch ovens and ranges in the United States, particularly models with full digital displays and touch controls.

### Common Causes of the E301 Error Code

Control board communication failures can be caused by:

- **Loose ribbon cable or harness connector** – The data cable connecting the UI board to the EOC board can work loose due to thermal expansion/contraction cycles in the oven environment. This is the most common and least expensive cause.
- **Heat-damaged wiring** – The communication wires can be damaged by heat radiating from the oven cavity, especially at points where they pass near the oven walls.
- **Failed EOC board** – The main oven control board can develop component failures that disable its communication circuit while other functions may or may not continue operating.
- **Failed UI board** – The user interface board can fail, losing its ability to communicate with the main board.
- **Power surge damage** – Electrical surges can damage the sensitive communication circuits on one or both boards without destroying the entire board.
- **Moisture damage** – Steam from cooking can rise and condense on the electronic boards, causing corrosion and short circuits on the communication traces.

### How to Troubleshoot the E301 Error Code

Try these steps:

1. **Power cycle the oven** – Turn off the circuit breaker for at least five minutes. This gives both boards time to fully discharge. When power is restored, the boards will attempt to re-establish communication.
2. **Check the display** – After restarting, observe whether the display lights up and shows any information. A completely blank display suggests the UI board or its power supply has failed. A garbled display suggests a communication issue.
3. **Try basic operations** – After reset, try setting the oven to bake at 350°F. If the oven heats normally, communication has been restored. If buttons don't respond or the display doesn't update, the communication link is still broken.
4. **Check for moisture** – If the oven was used for long, steamy cooking (boiling water, braising) and E301 appeared shortly after, moisture may be the cause. Leave the oven off with the door slightly open for 24 hours to dry.
5. **Inspect for power supply issues** – Ensure the oven is on a dedicated circuit and that the connection at the junction box is secure.

### When to Call a Professional

Persistent E301 errors require a technician to inspect the wiring harness, test both boards, and identify which component has failed. **Contact our Bosch oven repair team** for expert electronic diagnosis and repair using factory-certified Bosch parts.

### What Does Bosch Oven Error Code E401 Mean?

The E401 error code on a Bosch oven indicates an **interior light circuit failure**. While this may seem like a minor issue compared to heating or safety errors, the E401 signifies that the control board has detected an electrical fault in the oven light circuit — not simply a burned-out bulb. The control board monitors the light circuit's current draw, and E401 is triggered when the current reading is abnormal (either zero when the light should be on, or excessive, indicating a short circuit).

This error code appears on Bosch ovens and ranges sold in the United States with electronically monitored light circuits. On many models, the oven light can be controlled by the control panel and also activates automatically when the oven door is opened.

The E401 will not prevent the oven from cooking — it is a secondary system error. However, operating without a functional oven light makes it impossible to check on food without opening the door, which releases heat and affects cooking.

### Common Causes of the E401 Error Code

The oven light circuit can fault for several reasons:

- **Burned-out light bulb** – The simplest cause. Oven bulbs (typically 40W halogen appliance bulbs) have a limited lifespan and burn out over time. However, a standard burnout usually doesn't trigger E401 unless the control board is monitoring the circuit actively.
- **Shorted bulb socket** – The bulb socket inside the oven cavity can develop corrosion or carbon buildup from high heat, creating a short circuit that triggers E401.
- **Broken or corroded bulb contacts** – The metal contacts in the light socket can corrode or lose tension, creating an intermittent or open connection.
- **Damaged light wiring** – Wires to the light socket route through the oven's high-temperature zone and can deteriorate, with insulation cracking and conductors being exposed.
- **Wrong bulb type** – Installing a standard (non-appliance-rated) light bulb in the oven can cause the bulb to fail prematurely or draw incorrect current, triggering E401.
- **Light switch failure** – On models with a separate light switch (door switch or control panel switch), the switch itself can fail.

### How to Troubleshoot the E401 Error Code

The E401 often has a simple fix:

1. **Replace the bulb** – Turn off the oven and let it cool completely. Locate the light bulb (usually behind a glass cover in the oven ceiling or rear wall). Remove the cover, unscrew the old bulb, and replace it with a Bosch-approved 40W halogen oven bulb rated for high temperatures. Use a dry cloth or glove when handling halogen bulbs — skin oils can cause hot spots and premature failure.
2. **Check the bulb socket** – While the bulb is removed, inspect the socket for corrosion, discoloration, or damage. If the contacts appear blackened or corroded, the socket may need replacement.
3. **Reset the oven** – After replacing the bulb, turn off the circuit breaker for 30 seconds and restore power. Test the light by opening the oven door.
4. **Use the correct bulb** – Only use oven-rated appliance bulbs (typically 40W halogen, G9 base on many Bosch models). Standard household LED or incandescent bulbs are not rated for oven temperatures.

### When to Call a Professional

If E401 persists after bulb replacement, the light socket, wiring, or door switch may need professional attention. **Contact our Bosch oven repair team** for light circuit diagnosis and repair.
