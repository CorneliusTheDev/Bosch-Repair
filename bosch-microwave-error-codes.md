## Error Code F1 – Magnetron Failure

### What Does Bosch Microwave Error Code F1 Mean?

The F1 error code on a Bosch microwave indicates a **magnetron failure**. The magnetron is the core component of every microwave oven — it is the vacuum tube device that converts electrical energy into microwave radiation (at 2,450 MHz), which penetrates food and heats it by causing water molecules to vibrate. Without a functioning magnetron, the microwave cannot heat food at all.

When the microwave's control board detects that the magnetron is not drawing the expected current, is overheating, or is otherwise not functioning within normal parameters, it triggers the F1 fault code and disables heating to prevent damage to other components and potential safety hazards.

This error is found on Bosch over-the-range microwaves, built-in microwave ovens, and countertop models sold in the United States. When F1 is active, the microwave's fan, light, and turntable may still work, but no heat is produced regardless of the power level selected.

### Common Causes of the F1 Error Code

Magnetron failure can be caused by several factors:

- **Age and wear** – The magnetron has a finite lifespan, typically rated at a certain number of operating hours. In a heavily used household microwave, the magnetron can wear out after 7-10 years of regular use. The internal filament or cathode material depletes over time, reducing the tube's ability to generate microwave energy.
- **Running while empty** – Operating a microwave with nothing inside (no food or water to absorb the microwaves) causes the magnetron to absorb its own energy output, which can permanently damage it in minutes. This is one of the most common causes of premature magnetron failure.
- **Arcing events** – Metal objects, damaged microwave-safe containers, or foods with very high mineral content can cause arcing (visible sparks) inside the microwave. Repeated arcing events generate intense, localized heat that can crack the magnetron's antenna or degrade its internal components.
- **Overheating** – If the microwave's cooling fan has failed or its ventilation is blocked, the magnetron can overheat during operation. The magnetron has a thermal cut-out (thermoprotector) that permanently opens if the magnetron exceeds a safe temperature.
- **Power surge damage** – Electrical surges from lightning strikes or utility issues can damage the magnetron's power supply components (diode, capacitor) or the magnetron itself.
- **Diode or capacitor failure** – The high-voltage diode and capacitor that supply power to the magnetron can fail, preventing the magnetron from receiving the ~4,000V DC it needs to operate. The F1 error may appear even though the magnetron itself is intact.

### How to Troubleshoot the F1 Error Code

**Important safety warning:** The microwave's high-voltage capacitor stores a potentially lethal charge of up to 4,000 volts even when the microwave is unplugged. Never open the outer casing of a microwave oven unless you are a trained appliance technician who knows how to safely discharge the capacitor. Serious injury or death can result from contact with the charged capacitor.

With that critical safety note, these are the safe homeowner steps:

1. **Reset the microwave** – Unplug the microwave from the wall outlet for at least 60 seconds, then plug it back in. A temporary electronic fault may clear with a full power reset.
2. **Test with a cup of water** – After reset, place a microwave-safe cup of water inside the microwave and run it on full power for 60 seconds. If the water heats, the F1 may have been a temporary fault. If the water stays cold, the magnetron or its power supply components have failed.
3. **Check for arcing damage** – Open the microwave and inspect the interior. Look for burn marks, carbon deposits on the waveguide cover (the flat panel on the ceiling of the microwave cavity), or damage to the mica sheet. If you see significant burning, the waveguide cover needs replacement at minimum.
4. **Assess repair vs. replace** – Magnetron replacement is expensive — often $150-$300 for parts plus labor. For microwaves more than 7-8 years old, consider whether repair is economically sensible compared to replacement.

## Error Code F2 – Temperature Sensor Fault

### What Does Bosch Microwave Error Code F2 Mean?

The F2 error code on a Bosch microwave indicates a **temperature sensor (thermistor) fault**. Many Bosch microwave models — particularly combination microwave-convection ovens and over-the-range units sold in the United States — use an internal temperature sensor to enable sensor cooking modes. These modes automatically adjust cooking time and power based on the steam or temperature emitted by the food, stopping the cycle when the food reaches the desired doneness.

When the control board cannot read a valid signal from the temperature sensor — either because the sensor is open-circuit, short-circuit, or returning values outside the expected range — it triggers the F2 error. Depending on the model, F2 may disable only the sensor cooking modes while allowing manual time-based cooking to continue, or it may prevent all microwave operation.

### Common Causes of the F2 Error Code

The temperature sensor fault can result from:

- **Failed thermistor** – The NTC thermistor inside the microwave cavity degrades over time from exposure to heat, steam, and microwave energy. Open-circuit failure (infinite resistance) is the most common mode.
- **Moisture damage** – Steam from cooking can condense on the sensor and its connector, causing corrosion or short circuits. This is particularly common on microwaves installed above a range where cooking steam from the stovetop rises into the microwave.
- **Disconnected sensor wire** – The thin wires connecting the sensor to the control board can vibrate loose in the connector housing, especially in over-the-range models that experience vibration from the range below.
- **Physical damage to the sensor** – The sensor probe, located inside the microwave cavity, can be damaged if struck by cookware or during cleaning.
- **Control board input circuit failure** – The sensor reading circuit on the control board can develop faults due to power surges or component aging.

### How to Troubleshoot the F2 Error Code

Try these steps:

1. **Reset the microwave** – Unplug for 60 seconds and plug back in. A temporary moisture-related fault may clear after drying out.
2. **Allow the microwave to dry** – If heavy steam cooking was done before F2 appeared, leave the microwave door slightly open (unplugged) for several hours to allow moisture to evaporate from the sensor and connectors.
3. **Test manual cooking** – Try running the microwave on a manual time setting (not sensor mode). If it heats normally in manual mode, only the sensor cooking function is affected, confirming the temperature sensor has failed rather than the magnetron.
4. **Clean the interior** – Thoroughly clean the microwave interior, including the area around the sensor probe (if visible). Remove any food splatters from the sensor surface.
5. **Check sensor cooking settings** – Avoid using sensor cooking modes until the F2 is resolved, as inaccurate sensor readings can lead to undercooked or overcooked food.

### When to Call a Professional

The temperature sensor requires professional replacement. **Contact our Bosch microwave repair team** for sensor diagnosis and replacement using factory-certified Bosch parts.

## Error Code F3 – Door Switch Failure

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/microwave/f3-door-switch-failure/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Microwave Error Code F3 – Door Switch Failure | Causes & Fixes

**Meta Description:** Bosch microwave showing F3? This indicates a door switch failure. Learn the causes and solutions for the F3 door switch error on your Bosch microwave.

**Keywords:** Bosch microwave error code F3, Bosch microwave door switch failure, Bosch microwave F3 fix, Bosch microwave won't start, Bosch microwave door interlock, Bosch microwave door latch, Bosch microwave repair

---

### What Does Bosch Microwave Error Code F3 Mean?

The F3 error code on a Bosch microwave indicates a **door switch (interlock switch) failure**. Microwave door switches are arguably the most critical safety components in the entire appliance. Microwaves use multiple interlock switches (typically three) that form a system: the primary switch, secondary switch, and monitor switch. All must be in the correct position for the microwave to operate. The monitor switch is particularly important — if it detects that the primary switch has failed and is stuck in the closed (energized) position, the monitor switch creates a deliberate short circuit to blow the fuse, ensuring the microwave cannot operate and emit microwave radiation with the door open.

The F3 error appears when the control board's door interlock monitoring circuit detects an inconsistency in the switch states — for example, switches reporting conflicting positions, or a switch failing to change state when the door is opened or closed.

This error is found on Bosch over-the-range and built-in microwave models in the United States. When F3 is active, the microwave will not start for any function as a mandatory safety measure.

### Common Causes of the F3 Error Code

Door switch failures are caused by:

- **Worn switch contacts** – Each door switch has spring-loaded electrical contacts that press together or separate when the door is opened and closed. After thousands of open-close cycles, these contacts wear out, corrode, or become pitted, causing intermittent or permanent failure.
- **Broken door latch hook** – The plastic latch hook on the door that physically depresses the door switches can crack or break. When the latch cannot fully depress the switch plunger, the switch doesn't activate properly.
- **Bent or damaged switch actuator** – The small plastic piece that translates door movement into switch activation can be deformed by rough door handling or impact damage.
- **Failed monitor switch** – The monitor switch has a different failure mode than the primary and secondary switches — it can blow the microwave's fuse when it detects a primary switch failure. If your microwave's fuse blew, a failed primary switch combined with an activated monitor switch is the likely cause.
- **Misaligned door** – If the microwave door is misaligned due to a bent hinge or a warped frame, the latch hooks may not fully engage the switches.
- **Electrical connection issues** – Wires to the door switches can loosen from vibration.

### How to Troubleshoot the F3 Error Code

**Safety note:** Do not attempt to bypass or disable microwave door interlocks. They exist to prevent exposure to microwave radiation. Bypassing them is dangerous and illegal.

Safe homeowner steps:

1. **Reset the microwave** – Unplug for 60 seconds and plug back in. Test by opening and closing the door firmly, then starting a cook cycle.
2. **Inspect the door latch** – Open the microwave door and examine the plastic latch hooks on the door. Look for cracks, breaks, or damage. Also check the corresponding latch holes on the microwave frame for broken plastic pieces.
3. **Close the door firmly** – Ensure you are closing the microwave door with a definitive push that fully engages the latch. A half-closed door can leave the switches in an intermediate position.
4. **Check the door alignment** – Close the door and check whether it sits flush with the microwave frame all around. An uneven gap suggests a bent or misaligned door.
5. **Check for a blown fuse** – If the microwave is completely dead (no display, no response), a blown fuse may be the result of a monitor switch activation. This requires professional diagnosis.

### When to Call a Professional

Door switch replacement requires accessing the interior of the microwave, which involves the high-voltage system. This is a job for a trained technician. **Contact our Bosch microwave repair team** for professional door switch diagnosis and replacement.

## Error Code F4 – Convection Fan Motor Fault

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/microwave/f4-convection-fan-motor-fault/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Microwave Error Code F4 – Convection Fan Fault | Causes & Fixes

**Meta Description:** Bosch microwave showing F4 error? This indicates a convection fan motor fault. Learn the causes and how to fix the F4 fan error on your Bosch microwave convection oven.

**Keywords:** Bosch microwave error code F4, Bosch microwave convection fan fault, Bosch microwave F4 fix, Bosch microwave fan not working, Bosch convection microwave repair, Bosch microwave oven repair, Bosch microwave repair

---

### What Does Bosch Microwave Error Code F4 Mean?

The F4 error code on a Bosch microwave indicates a **convection fan motor fault**. This error specifically applies to Bosch combination microwave-convection ovens, which are common in the United States market as built-in models and over-the-range units. In convection mode, the microwave uses a heating element and a fan to circulate hot air throughout the cavity, allowing it to function like a regular oven for baking, roasting, and broiling.

When the control board activates the convection fan and detects that the fan is not running (through current monitoring or a tachometer signal), is running below expected speed, or has stalled after starting, it triggers F4. Without the convection fan, the heating element cannot be used safely because heat would concentrate in one area and potentially damage the cavity or create fire hazards.

The F4 error will disable convection cooking modes, though standard microwave modes may continue to function.

### Common Causes of the F4 Error Code

The convection fan can malfunction for several reasons:

- **Motor burnout** – The fan motor that drives the convection fan operates in an extremely hot environment during convection cooking. The bearings can seize, the windings can overheat, or electrical components can fail after extended use.
- **Food debris or grease on the fan** – Splattered food and grease can accumulate on the fan blades over time, adding weight and creating imbalance. Heavy accumulation can slow the fan or cause the motor to stall under the increased load.
- **Foreign object in the fan** – A small piece of food, foil, or packaging that entered the fan area can jam the blades.
- **Wiring issues** – Wires to the fan motor can be damaged by heat from the convection element or loosened at their connectors.
- **Motor thermal protection tripped** – The fan motor has an internal thermal cut-out that opens if the motor overheats. This can happen if the motor is struggling against a load (debris on blades) or if a previous convection cycle ran very long.
- **Control board fan relay failure** – The relay that powers the convection fan can fail on the control board.

### How to Troubleshoot the F4 Error Code

Try these steps:

1. **Clean the microwave interior thoroughly** – Use microwave-safe cleaning solutions to degrease the interior walls, ceiling, and any visible fan covers. Heavy grease buildup can reach the fan blades.
2. **Reset the microwave** – Unplug for 60 seconds and allow the fan motor to cool. Restore power and test convection mode with a short test run (5 minutes at 350°F).
3. **Listen for the fan** – When you start convection mode, listen for the fan spinning immediately behind the rear wall of the cavity. Silence or grinding sounds indicate a fan problem.
4. **Test microwave mode** – If standard microwave cooking works but convection mode triggers F4, the fault is isolated to the convection fan or its circuit.
5. **Avoid running convection mode** – Until repaired, avoid convection mode to prevent potential overheating of the cavity.

### When to Call a Professional

The convection fan is located inside the microwave housing and requires disassembly to access. **Contact our Bosch microwave repair team** for professional fan motor replacement using factory-certified Bosch parts.

## Error Code F5 – Control Board Failure

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/microwave/f5-control-board-failure/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Microwave Error Code F5 – Control Board Failure | Causes & Fixes

**Meta Description:** Bosch microwave displaying F5? This indicates a control board failure. Learn the causes and how to fix the F5 electronic fault on your Bosch microwave.

**Keywords:** Bosch microwave error code F5, Bosch microwave control board failure, Bosch microwave F5 fix, Bosch microwave electronic fault, Bosch microwave not responding, Bosch microwave control panel, Bosch microwave repair

---

### What Does Bosch Microwave Error Code F5 Mean?

The F5 error code on a Bosch microwave indicates a **main control board failure**. The control board is the electronic brain of the microwave — it processes all user inputs from the control panel, manages the cook cycles, controls the magnetron power levels, operates the turntable, controls the door interlocks, and manages all timing functions. A control board failure can manifest in many ways, from a completely dead microwave to erratic behavior, incorrect power levels, or specific function failures.

The F5 code specifically indicates that the control board has performed a self-diagnostic check and detected an internal fault in its own circuitry. This is different from errors like F2 (sensor fault) or F3 (door switch) which indicate failures in peripheral components — F5 means the problem is with the board itself.

This error appears on Bosch microwave models sold in the United States, particularly newer models with advanced electronic controls and multiple cooking modes.

### Common Causes of the F5 Error Code

Control board failures can be caused by:

- **Power surge damage** – The single most common cause of sudden control board failure. A voltage spike from a lightning strike, utility surge, or even an appliance on the same circuit starting up can destroy sensitive electronic components on the board.
- **Moisture or steam damage** – Steam from cooking rises and can condense on the control board, especially in over-the-range installations above a steaming pot. Moisture causes short circuits and corrosion on circuit board traces.
- **Component aging** – Electronic components like capacitors, resistors, and transistors degrade over years of use. Failed capacitors are particularly common — they can cause the board to behave erratically before failing completely.
- **Overheating** – If the microwave's internal cooling fan fails, excess heat can damage the control board's components.
- **Voltage irregularities** – Consistently low or high voltage from a poorly wired outlet or an undersized circuit can stress the board's power supply components over time.
- **Physical impact** – Severe vibration (in over-the-range models with a high-power ventilation fan) or impact to the microwave can damage solder joints or board-mounted components.

### How to Troubleshoot the F5 Error Code

Options for homeowners are limited with control board failures:

1. **Hard reset the microwave** – Unplug the microwave for at least five minutes (longer than a standard reset). This gives the board's capacitors time to fully discharge. Plug back in and check if F5 clears.
2. **Check the outlet** – Plug another appliance into the same outlet to verify the outlet is providing power. If the outlet works, the problem is in the microwave itself.
3. **Check for a surge protector** – If the microwave is not on a surge protector and a power event recently occurred (storm, flickering lights), a surge may have damaged the board. For future protection, use a high-quality surge protector rated for microwave appliances.
4. **Assess the display** – Note what the display shows when F5 appears. A completely blank display suggests a power supply failure on the board. A garbled or partially lit display suggests the display driver circuit has failed.
5. **Check for burning smell** – If you detect a burning electronic smell from the microwave, immediately unplug it and do not use it. This suggests a component on the board has burned.
6. **Consider age and repair cost** – Control board replacement is expensive. For microwaves over 8 years old, compare the cost of a new control board (typically $150-$350 plus labor) against the cost of a new microwave.

### When to Call a Professional

Control board diagnosis and replacement requires disassembling the microwave and working near high-voltage components. **Contact our Bosch microwave repair team** for control board diagnosis and replacement using factory-certified Bosch parts.

## Error Code F7 – Keypad or Touch Panel Fault

**Page URL:** `/error-codes/microwave/f7-keypad-touch-panel-fault/`

**SEO Title:** Bosch Microwave Error Code F7 – Keypad Fault | Causes & Fixes

**Keywords:** Bosch microwave error code F7, Bosch microwave keypad fault, Bosch microwave F7 fix, Bosch microwave buttons not working, Bosch microwave touch panel, Bosch microwave control panel error, Bosch microwave repair

### What Does Bosch Microwave Error Code F7 Mean?

The F7 error code on a Bosch microwave indicates a **keypad or touch panel fault**. The control panel on a Bosch microwave — whether it uses physical membrane buttons or a capacitive touch panel — connects to the main control board to relay your cooking instructions. The F7 error is triggered when the control board detects that a key or sensor on the panel is stuck in the activated state (continuously reporting as pressed), that the panel is not responding to inputs, or that the panel's electronic signals are out of the expected range.

When a key is continuously reporting as pressed (stuck key), the microwave cannot function safely because the key input would interfere with every operation. For example, a stuck number key would make it impossible to enter cooking times accurately, and a stuck start button could cause the microwave to start unexpectedly.

This error is found on Bosch microwave models with electronic control panels sold in the United States. The F7 may render the microwave completely non-functional or may disable specific buttons while others continue working.

### Common Causes of the F7 Error Code

Keypad and touch panel faults are most commonly caused by:

- **Moisture under the membrane** – The most frequent cause. Steam from cooking, condensation, or a direct liquid spill can seep under the membrane keypad and cause individual keys to short-circuit in the pressed position.
- **Grease and food buildup** – Heavy grease accumulation on and around membrane keys can physically hold the membrane depressed, creating a stuck-key condition.
- **Cracked or damaged membrane** – The flexible membrane that forms the keypad surface can crack, especially at hinge points around frequently used keys like Start and Time Cook. A cracked membrane can allow moisture inside or cause the contact points to permanently close.
- **Worn keypad contacts** – The conductive carbon contacts on the membrane or keypad PCB wear down with use. After many years, the contacts may lose their resistance consistency, causing erratic readings.
- **Touch panel circuit failure** – On touch panel models, the capacitive sensor chip can fail, misidentifying "no touch" conditions as "touch" conditions.
- **Harsh cleaning** – Using excessive liquid or harsh cleaning sprays directly on the control panel can drive moisture under the membrane.

### How to Troubleshoot the F7 Error Code

Try these steps to address the keypad fault:

1. **Clean the control panel** – With the microwave unplugged, wipe the entire control panel surface with a barely damp cloth. Remove all grease, food splatters, and residue from around every button. Pay special attention to the edges of each button.
2. **Reset the microwave** – Unplug for five minutes to clear the stuck-key detection. When power is restored, the board re-reads all key states. If the cause was temporary moisture that has since dried, the F7 may not return.
3. **Dry the panel area** – If moisture is suspected (steam, splash, or high-humidity kitchen), leave the microwave unplugged with the door open for several hours. Gently warm air from a hair dryer (on low heat, from a distance of 12+ inches) can help evaporate moisture from under the membrane.
4. **Test each button** – After reset, methodically press each button on the control panel. Note which buttons do not respond or which cause unexpected inputs. This helps identify whether the problem is widespread (board failure) or isolated (specific stuck key).
5. **Avoid using the faulty button** – If a specific button is stuck, you may be able to temporarily work around it by using other buttons to program the same function.
6. **Do not spray cleaners on the panel** – Going forward, apply cleaning products to a cloth first, never directly to the control panel.

### When to Call a Professional

Membrane keypads and touch panels are typically replaced as a complete assembly. **Contact our Bosch microwave repair team** for keypad diagnosis and replacement using factory-certified Bosch parts.

## Error Code F9 – Cooling Fan Fault

**SEO Title:** Bosch Microwave Error Code F9 – Cooling Fan Fault | Causes & Fixes

**Keywords:** Bosch microwave error code F9, Bosch microwave cooling fan fault, Bosch microwave F9 fix, Bosch microwave fan not running, Bosch microwave overheating, Bosch microwave ventilation, Bosch microwave repair

### What Does Bosch Microwave Error Code F9 Mean?

The F9 error code on a Bosch microwave indicates a **cooling fan fault**. The microwave has an internal cooling fan (separate from any external ventilation fan in over-the-range models) that draws air across the magnetron, high-voltage transformer, and control board to prevent them from overheating during operation. This fan is critical for the longevity of the microwave's most expensive and heat-sensitive components.

When the control board detects that the cooling fan is not running, is running too slowly, or has stalled, it triggers the F9 error. In many cases, the microwave will limit cooking power or stop operation entirely to prevent component damage from overheating.

This error is found on Bosch built-in and over-the-range microwave models sold in the United States. The cooling fan should start whenever the microwave is in use and typically continues running for a short period after cooking ends to cool the magnetron and transformer.

### Common Causes of the F9 Error Code

The cooling fan can fail for these reasons:

- **Seized or worn motor bearings** – The fan motor's bearings can dry out and seize after years of operation, preventing the fan from spinning. Before complete seizure, you may hear a grinding or rattling noise from inside the microwave.
- **Debris on the fan blades** – Grease, dust, and food particles can accumulate on the fan blades and in the fan housing, slowing the fan or causing it to stall under load.
- **Failed fan motor winding** – The motor's electrical windings can burn out due to overheating, manufacturing defect, or age.
- **Wiring connection failure** – The wires connecting the fan motor to the control board can loosen at their connectors, cutting power to the fan.
- **Blocked airflow** – On over-the-range models, the ventilation ducting can become clogged with grease, reducing the airflow that the internal fan depends on. This can cause the fan motor to work harder and eventually overheat.
- **Control board fan circuit failure** – The relay or transistor on the control board that controls the fan motor can fail.

### How to Troubleshoot the F9 Error Code

Try these steps before scheduling repair:

1. **Listen for the fan** – Start a short cook cycle and listen for the internal cooling fan. It should start immediately when cooking begins. If you hear grinding, rattling, or buzzing, the fan is struggling. If you hear nothing, the fan has likely failed.
2. **Check for accessible obstructions** – On over-the-range models, check the grease filter(s) (usually removable panels on the underside of the microwave). Heavily clogged grease filters restrict airflow. Remove and wash them.
3. **Reset the microwave** – Unplug for 60 seconds and restore power. A temporary motor stall or electronic fault may clear.
4. **Clean the grease filters** – Remove the filters and wash them in hot, soapy water or run them through the dishwasher. Reinstall when dry.
5. **Check the vent path** – If the over-the-range model is ducted to the outside, ensure the exterior vent hood is not blocked by debris, bird nests, or ice.
6. **Avoid extended cooking** – Until repaired, limit continuous cooking times to prevent magnetron overheating without adequate cooling.

### When to Call a Professional

The internal cooling fan is located inside the microwave housing and requires disassembly near high-voltage components to access. **Contact our Bosch microwave repair team** for professional cooling fan diagnosis and replacement using factory-certified Bosch parts.
